# Imported models

glTF, FBX and OBJ meshes, texture maps, and SurfaceAppearance.

- **Draw list:** `Shape::Mesh(Arc<dyn MeshSource>)` is a mesh the caller owns (positions, normals, optional texture coordinates, `u32` indices), and `Material::base_color_texture` an `Arc<dyn TextureSource>` (sRGB RGBA8). They're traits so `scene` hands over the loaded model's data without copying it each frame. The renderer uploads each on first sight, keyed by `key()`, and frees what hasn't been drawn for 600 frames and 10 seconds (`KEEP_UNDRAWN_FRAMES`, `KEEP_UNDRAWN_SECONDS`: at 144 fps, 600 frames alone was four seconds, and turning back toward something re-uploaded it). **Kept within a budget** (ADR-0051, `renderer/undrawn.rs`): a renderer that shows a world to someone, the player's window and the editor's viewport, is told to keep them instead (`set_keep_undrawn`). Meshes then stay while they fit the mesh memory budget (`EffectiveGraphics::mesh_budget`, half the texture memory budget) and color textures while they fit the texture memory budget; past a budget, those undrawn that long are freed, the least recently drawn first and no more than it takes, before any texture being drawn loses a level ([shadows.md](shadows.md)). What is being drawn is never freed. So a cut or a turn back to what was left a while ago uploads nothing, and memory in use grows toward the budgets as a world is seen. Materials, UI images and terrain weights leave after the 10 seconds either way (small, and some are new every frame), and other renderers (thumbnails, bakes, previews) keep the 10-second rule for everything. With `set_background_uploads` on (streaming, and always in the editor's viewport) uploads run on a thread (`renderer/uploads.rs`), except terrain chunks' meshes and weights, which go up in the frame that first draws them (a chunk changing level would otherwise leave a hole for a few frames) and `TextureRenderer::preload` asks for every item's mesh, levels of detail and textures, drawn or not. Broken data (an index past the last corner, the wrong number of pixels) is logged once and not drawn.
- **Vertices** gained texture coordinates (location 12). Built-in shapes carry real ones: a box face runs 0–1 along its edges, a cylinder's side goes once around its circumference, and a sphere covers its surface. Tangents go with them. Textures are bound as group 1 with a repeating, mipmapped linear sampler; an item with no texture binds a 1×1 white texture. Mipmaps are made on the CPU at upload by 2×2 averaging (in sRGB, slightly off but not visibly). Levels bigger than the GPU allows are left out, so huge textures show less sharp instead of failing.
- **Model space:** `assets` fits every model into the 1×1×1 cube centered on the origin, like the built-ins, so `Size` stretches its box. It walks the default scene's node tree (or every mesh, if there's no scene), applying node transforms (mirrored nodes get their triangles flipped back); missing normals are made smooth from the triangles. Only triangle lists are drawn; points, lines, strips and fans are skipped.
- **FBX** files are read by `ufbx`, which converts them to glTF's space (Y up, right-handed, meters) whatever axes and units they were saved in, and triangulates polygons. Every visible node with a mesh is drawn, at its world transform (pivots and geometry offsets included), one draw item per material it uses. Normals the file lacks are made by `ufbx`. Materials become metallic-roughness: `ufbx` maps Lambert/Phong and other FBX shading models (and Blender's Principled BSDF) to PBR values; base color × factor, opacity (below 1 is `BLEND`; an opacity map makes it `MASK` at 0.5), metalness (default 0), roughness (default 1, Phong shininess converted), emission × factor, and the base color texture with V flipped to the engine's top-left origin. Their other maps are read too (see Texture maps). Skeletons, skin weights and takes are read too (see Animation). Not yet: per-UV-set texture choice (the first UV set is used).
- **OBJ** files (P7.1) are read by the same `ufbx` code as FBX (`fbx::Source::Obj`): the `.mtl` library their first `mtllib` line names is read from the model's folder and handed to `ufbx` (`obj_mtl_data`); its `Kd`, `Ks`, `Ns`, `d`, `Ke` and `map_*` lines become the same metallic-roughness materials, with textures looked for as an FBX's are. OBJ has no axes or units: it's taken as Y up, 1 unit = 1 stud. Each `o` object is an object for picks and the Import dialog's Objects tab. A library that isn't there leaves the model drawn in grey, with a warning. A model whose library is there isn't kept as a cooked `.axmodel` (the cooked copy's stamp covers only the `.obj`), so editing the `.mtl` shows on the next load.
- **Materials:** each glTF primitive is one draw item with its metallic-roughness material: base color factor × base color texture, metalness, roughness, emissive (converted from glTF's linear light to the draw list's sRGB). Alpha mode `OPAQUE` is solid, `MASK` cuts out pixels below its cutoff (`Material::alpha_cutoff`, a `discard` in the shader; under TAA, far off, where its mips have averaged the alpha, the share covered is kept instead: see Cut-outs far off in [textures.md](textures.md)), `BLEND` is drawn see-through (opacity capped just below 1 so it always takes the see-through path). Solid items always write alpha 1. Its metallic-roughness, normal, occlusion and emissive textures are used (see Texture maps). Skins are read (see Rig in [animation.md](animation.md)). Animations are read as takes (see Takes in [animation.md](animation.md)). Not yet: vertex colors, `KHR_materials_emissive_strength`.
- **Skin**: a material with `"axcenity_shading": "skin"` in its glTF `extras` is drawn with the Skin shading model, and `"axcenity_defined_normal": {"index": <texture>}` names its second normal map ([skin.md](skin.md)). Other values, or none, are Standard.
- **Hair and cloth** (CO7): `"axcenity_shading": "hair"` draws a material with the Hair model; a hair card atlas names its data and flow maps in the same `extras` (`axcenity_hair_data`, `axcenity_hair_flow`), and `axcenity_hair_shell: true` marks a layer lying on the head a shell ([hair.md](hair.md)). `"axcenity_shading": "cloth"` draws it with the Cloth model, whose sheen and anisotropy are read from glTF's `KHR_materials_sheen` and `KHR_materials_anisotropy` (their factors; [cloth.md](cloth.md)). A Standard material ignores both extensions.
- **Translucent leaves**: a material with `axcenity_flutter` in its glTF `extras` is a leaf: it lets `LEAF_TRANSLUCENCY` (0.42) of the light from behind it through (`Material::translucency`, `through_leaf` in shader.wgsl), glowing a yellower green, and reflects correspondingly less (`LEAF_REFLECTION_LOSS`); what comes through is never more than what the leaf doesn't reflect (`through_thin` caps it, so a pale leaf with the green tint can't give back more light than reaches it; before 2026-11-26 it could give back half as much again). `axcenity_translucency` (0 to 1) sets it for any material; 0 turns it off. A translucent material is lit as foliage (`lit_leaf`): sky light follows where the leaf is in its plant rather than which way its card faces, by bending the normal toward the outside of a ball around the item (`leaf_canopy`, `CANOPY_BEND`) and shading its inside (`CANOPY_DEPTH_SHADE`); sun light stays per card, so each leaf catches it on its own. Leaves are kept from mirroring a white sky at grazing angles by a minimum roughness (`LEAF_ROUGHNESS_OUTSIDE`, `_INSIDE`), and `leaf_tone` varies each card's brightness and hue from the way it faces. Three things make a plant sit in its light (2026-11-23):
  - **Low plants are lit like the ground they stand on.** A plant under 3 studs tall has `LEAF_GROUND_LIFT` (0.8) added to the height of its bent normal before it's made unit length, fading out by 9 studs (`LEAF_GROUND_HEIGHTS`), so grass and low brush take the sun and the sky much as the ground does and a tree's crown keeps its own shape. Lit by the way its upright cards face, grass took little of a high sun and most of the horizon's glow, so it looked dull at noon and glowed at dusk while the ground round it went dark. A plant's albedo should then be what the plant's is: a straw painted pale to make up for the old lighting comes out too bright.
  - **A plant is darker toward its foot** (`LEAF_FOOT_SHADE` 0.55 of the surrounding light at the ground, whole from `LEAF_FOOT_REACH` 0.5 of its height up), where its own leaves and stems stand in the way. Ground cover is shaded more (`LEAF_FOOT_SHADE_LOW` 0.3, blended in as the plant is lower, by `LEAF_GROUND_HEIGHTS`; 2026-11-26), so a tuft of grass on asphalt is dark where it meets the ground instead of looking pasted on. Where in its plant a point is comes from the item's box (a model's every primitive shares the model's), since models carry no vertex colours.
  - **The sky comes through a leaf from its far side** (2026-11-26): `through_thin` reads the light round the leaf (light probe volumes, then the sky's harmonics, as reflected light does) the way the leaf's far side faces in its crown, `-mix(normal, crown, CANOPY_BEND)`, tipped up by `LEAF_SKY_WRAP` (0.35) since light comes down through a crown from leaf to leaf. So a crown seen from below glows with the overcast sky behind it, and so does the middle of a tuft of grass with the sky behind it. Before, the far side was the reflected light's, lifted for low plants (`LEAF_GROUND_LIFT`), so a tuft's far side faced the ground: translucent grass let through the ground's dark and came out darker than grass letting nothing through.
  - **Leaves take half of screen-space ambient occlusion** (`LEAF_SCREEN_OCCLUSION`, an exponent of 0.5 on it; 2026-11-26). The prepass sees a crown as a heap of solid cards, so every leaf in it was darkened as if deep in a solid ball, on top of the crown's own shade (`CANOPY_DEPTH_SHADE`, the foot's): at the Overgrown Street showcase's `AmbientOcclusionIntensity` (1.6) crowns went near black. Leaves still occlude what's round them (the ground under a bush) as before; only what they receive changed. An exponent of 0.35 flattened a forest seen from above.
  - Tests: `renderer/tests/foliage_light.rs` (a crown of 900 cards keeps over 0.82 of its light under ambient occlusion, at least 0.1 more than the same cards solid; a crown seen from below glows with the sky through it; the middle and foot of a low card with the sky behind it are brighter letting light through than not; a low plant's foot is under 0.65 of its top; a pale leaf under an even white sky gives back no more than a white card; a thin post's, a barrier's and a tuft's foot darken the ground). Before the change four of them failed. Cost: a power, a minimum and one more direction per leaf pixel, no pass, binding or attribute; the player's bench of the Overgrown Street showcase (Ultra, 2560 × 1440, RX 9070 XT, before and after alternated) measured the scene pass 9.08–9.13 ms before and 9.12–9.13 after, its scattered items 3.53–3.56 ms both, 44.4–45.8 frames a second shown before and 43.7–45.2 after: within noise (2026-11-26).
  - **Glossy leaves** (2026-11-27): `axcenity_glossy_leaf` (`true` or a number above 0) in a material's glTF `extras` marks a leaf with a sheen of its own, such as ivy or laurel (`ModelMaterial::glossy_leaf`, `Material::glossy_leaf`, `MapsUniform::shading.z`): `lit_leaf` lights it at its own roughness (a map's or factor's) instead of raising it to the leaf's least, and where it hangs it's lit as a leaf, not as thin cloth. The cooked model cache keeps it in the hanging flag's byte (bit 1), so older caches read the same. Overgrown Street's ivy has it (its atlas's ivy is 0.34 rough). Tests: `a_glossy_leaf_keeps_its_own_sheen_and_a_hanging_one_is_still_a_leaf` (`renderer/tests/foliage_light.rs`: a 0.1-rough crown's highlights come back three times as bright, the flag changes nothing on a rough leaf, and a hanging glossy crown draws as a standing one), `a_cooked_model_keeps_a_hanging_glossy_leaf`, and the extras' reading in `assets`.
- **Hanging cloth isn't a leaf.** A translucent material on something that hangs (`axcenity_hang`, [wind.md](wind.md)), unless it's a glossy leaf, is lit by `lit_thin_cloth`: by the way it faces, in its own colors, with the light that comes through it left untinted (`through_thin` with a white tint; a leaf's is `THROUGH_LEAF_TINT`). Before, a drape with a translucency glowed yellow-green in the sun.
- **Wind**: `axcenity_sway` and `axcenity_flutter` in a glTF material's `extras` make what's drawn with it bend and shiver in the wind, and `axcenity_hang` makes it sway from its top down, as drapes do ([wind.md](wind.md)).
- **Shared embedded textures**: a glb's embedded image is decoded once per distinct set of encoded bytes in a project (`EMBEDDED_IMAGES` in `assets/src/model.rs`, held weakly; not across projects, since a cooked image reads its pixels back from its own project), so a pack that embeds the same texture in many models loads it and uploads it once.
- **Material files** (graphics Phase 7; `assets/src/material.rs`, `scene/src/material.rs`): a `*.material.json` file (the editor saves them in `assets/materials`) holds one standard material: `color` and `emissiveColor` (sRGB, 0 to 1, like a Part's `Color`), `metalness`, `roughness`, `emissiveStrength` (the glow is `emissiveColor` times it), `opacity` (below 1 is see-through), `alphaCutoff` (above 0, cut out below it), `maps` (`color`, `normal`, `metalness`, `roughness`, `occlusion`, `emissive`, `opacity`, `specular`, each `{ "path", "channel", "invert", "strength", "directX" }`: `channel` is `"Red"`…`"Alpha"` for the one-channel maps, `invert` reads a glossiness or inverted-opacity map, `strength` is the normal map's scale and the occlusion's strength, `directX` flips a normal map's green), `tiling` and `offset` (`[u, v]`, as a SurfaceAppearance's `Tiling` and `Offset`), `doubleSided`, `sway` (how much what's drawn with it sways in the wind, 0 for still: [wind.md](wind.md)), `water` (makes it a water material, its values merged one by one: [water.md](water.md)), `shading` and `toon` (Standard, Unlit or Toon, and toon's bands, ramp, softness, rim and highlight: [stylized.md](stylized.md)), `outline` (a line around its parts' silhouettes, `width` and `color`: [stylized.md](stylized.md)), `shader` (WGSL code; see Surface shaders below; `""` takes away the base's), `parameters` (values the shader reads by name, see Surface shaders) and `graph` (the editor's material graph the shader was made from; see Material graphs below). A metalness or roughness map multiplies its value, as glTF's do; an opacity map shows only with an `opacity` below 1 or an `alphaCutoff`. A file names only what it sets (`MaterialFile`, every value an `Option`). `base` names another material file by its project path: each value the file leaves out comes from the base (and its base), so an instance overrides some values and follows its base for the rest. A map is merged value by value, so an instance can change a map's strength and keep its image, and `"path": ""` takes away a map the base has. What no file sets is the default material (`StandardMaterial::default`: white plastic, roughness 0.5, no glow, no maps). `Assets::material` reads a file and its bases once and keeps the result (`StandardMaterial`); `Assets::forget` of any file in the chain rereads it, and `forget_failures` retries one whose base was missing. `Assets::put_material_file` uses a file's edited contents in place of the file (the editor's Material tab hands each edit over; `None` reads the file again), forgetting every material built on it, and `Assets::material_chain` gives a file's chain of bases and their values put together. A material file that can't be read is reported once ("Could not load the material `…`: …") and the part draws as if it had none. A missing base, or bases that lead back around (`a → b → a`, or a file naming itself), is reported once and the default material stands in for that base; the files already read still apply. A Part's or MeshPart's **`MaterialVariant`** (a string, empty for none) names the file. On a Part it replaces the `Material` preset and its `Metalness`, `Roughness` and `Emission`; its `Color` tints the material (multiplies its color), `Transparency` still fades it and `TileSize` still sets the studs per repeat when it has maps. On a MeshPart it replaces every material of the model. A SurfaceAppearance wins over it: a Part with one ignores its `MaterialVariant`, and in a MeshPart the appearance's material keeps the appearance. Material files are new optional files, so the project format doesn't change; an exported game carries them with the rest of `assets`, and the player reads them (and their maps) when it first draws. The editor's loading screen reads them ahead (`AssetFile::Material`). `scene::part_material_file` makes a file from a Part's look (its preset, properties and SurfaceAppearance; a Neon part's glow is saved at the bloom boost; a part already using a material file gets an instance of it, overriding the color by its tint), for the editor's Save as Material (`docs/editor/assets.md`).
- **Surface shaders** (graphics Phase 22; `render/src/renderer/surface_shaders.rs`): a material file's `shader` is WGSL code with a `fn surface(input: SurfaceInput) -> Surface` that works out each pixel's surface in place of the standard one. `SurfaceInput` has `uv` (after the material's tiling and offset), `world_position`, `normal` (the mesh's, unit length, facing the camera, before the normal map), `tangent` (along U, `w` the side V is on), `time` (seconds, `DrawList::time`: the player counts the frames run, the editor the seconds since it started, in the viewport, Play and the Material tab's preview), and the item's own `color`, `opacity`, `metalness`, `roughness` and `emissive` before its maps. `Surface` is `base_color`, `metalness`, `roughness`, `normal` (world space), `emissive` (added after lighting) and `opacity` (below the cutoff cuts the pixel out; an item is see-through only when its material's opacity is below 1). Colors are linear light: `srgb(color)` turns a color picked as sRGB into it. `parameter(i)` reads the material's `parameters` (graphics Phase 23): named numbers (`"Amount": 0.5`, in `x`) or colors and vectors (`"Tint": [1, 0.5, 0]`, sRGB, in `xyz`), `i` counting from 0 in name order, up to 8 (`SURFACE_PARAMETERS`). The file with the shader names them; an instance sets their values (merged name by name, like maps), and a value for a name the shader's file doesn't have is left out, so the others keep their places. They reach the shader in the material's uniform (`Material::surface_parameters`, `MapsUniform::params`), not its code, so materials that differ only in them share one pipeline. `standard_surface(input)` is the material's own look at `input.uv`, so a shader usually changes its input or its result (crates/samples/projects/shader scrolls its conveyor's chevrons: `moved.uv = input.uv - vec2<f32>(input.time * 0.5, 0.0)`). The ambient occlusion and specular maps stay at the mesh's coordinates. The code may define other functions and constants, but must sample textures (`standard_surface` does) where every pixel runs together, not inside a branch, as WGSL requires. `scene` hands it over as `Material::surface_shader` (`SurfaceShader`, named by the material file the code is in, so an instance's error is on its base's lines). The renderer puts it after the scene shader's template (`SCENE_TEMPLATE`: sky, lighting and shader.wgsl; the standard surface is surface.wgsl in the same place), so its line *n* is always the template's line count plus *n*. It's checked with `naga` (from `wgpu`) first, then compiled into its own scene and prepass pipelines inside wgpu error scopes, once per distinct code (`SurfaceShader::key`, a hash, so materials with the same code share them), compiled again when the samples per pixel change, and freed after 600 undrawn frames. Shadows use the standard pipelines. A shader that doesn't compile is logged and handed over once (`TextureRenderer::take_shader_errors`, `Renderer::take_shader_errors`: `ShaderError` with the material, the line of its code and the message, naga's `[n]` expression numbers replaced by their code), and its items draw with surface_error.wgsl's glowing magenta and black checks; everything else draws as usual. The player prints them as errors, the editor shows them in the Output window, and the every-example test fails on any.
- **Material graphs** (graphics Phase 23; `editor/src/material_graph.rs`): the editor's Material tab can build a material's surface from nodes (`docs/editor/assets.md`). The graph is kept in the file's `graph` (a node graph, as the UI Event Graph's are kept) and is compiled in the editor to the file's `shader`, `parameters` and maps, which are written beside it: the player reads only those, never the graph, and has no graph code. Graphs never become Lua, and gameplay graphs never become WGSL. Nodes: Material Output (Base Color, Metalness, Roughness, Normal, Emissive and Opacity; an unwired input keeps the material's own value, without maps), Material Input (the part's color times the material's, and its values), UV, Tiling and Offset, World Position, Normal, Time, Texture Sample (an image, read as Color, so linear light, or Data, as it is), Number, Color (sRGB, as picked), Make Vector2/3, Split, Add, Subtract, Multiply, Divide, Power, Minimum, Maximum, Mix, One Minus, Sine, Absolute, Fraction, Clamp 0 to 1, Smooth Step, Blend (Mix, Multiply, Screen, Overlay, Add), Hue Shift, Contrast, Noise (Perlin, Value, Voronoi; 0 to 1 over UV) and Number and Color Parameters (each a `parameters` entry by its Name). Values are numbers, Vector2s and Vector3s; math nodes take any of them and give back what they're given, and a number fills a vector. A graph's images go in the map slots its shader reads them from: colors in the color and glow slots (sRGB views), then the metalness, roughness, opacity and normal slots (read as they are, so a color there is turned into linear light in the shader), up to 6. Its maps replace the file's, but for the occlusion and specular maps, which still light it as usual; an instance's graph empties the slots it leaves free of its base's maps. An instance of a graph material can change the parameters, and swap an image by setting that slot's map. `compile` reports errors by node: no Material Output (on the graph), a second one, an output with nothing wired in, an input that needs a value, a type mismatch (on the node where the types meet: `A is a Vector2 but B is a Vector3`, `Base Color needs a Vector3, but is given a Vector2`), a value that doesn't read, an image not picked, too many images or parameters, two parameters with one name, and a value depending on itself. crates/samples/projects/material-graph mixes stone and moss by Perlin noise, with the moss tinted by a Color Parameter that its second material (an instance) overrides.
- **Texture placement and sides** (graphics Phase 6): `Material::uv_scale`, `uv_rotation` (radians) and `uv_offset` place every map of an item. The vertex shader applies them after `DrawItem::uv_tile`: scale, then turn (counter-clockwise in a texture whose V runs down, as glTF's `KHR_texture_transform`), then offset. They're in `MapsUniform` (`uv_matrix`, `uv_offset`), so items that differ in them get their own material binding; untextured items ignore them. `Material::double_sided` draws an item with pipelines that don't cull back faces (a solid and a see-through one); the fragment shader turns the normal, and the normal map's frame, to face the camera on a back face (`front_facing`), so a back is lit as a front. Batches are split by sidedness. Shadows already draw both sides. glTF reads `doubleSided` and `KHR_texture_transform` (offset, rotation, scale) from the first of the base color, metallic-roughness and emissive textures that has one; one transform places all of a primitive's maps, and a transform that moves its texture to another coordinate set (`texCoord`) is left out. FBX reads the base color map's (else the normal map's) placement from `ufbx`'s `uv_to_texture`: its scale and translation (the inverse of the texture's own `Scaling` and `Translation`), with V flipped to the engine's top-left origin; rotation is left out, and FBX has no double-sided flag.
- **Texture maps** (Phase 19): `Material::maps` (`TextureMaps`) adds a normal map (`NormalMap`: scale, and `flip_green` for DirectX-style maps), metalness, roughness, ambient occlusion and opacity maps (`ChannelMap`: one channel of a texture, optionally inverted, so a glossiness or smoothness map works as roughness and one packed texture can serve several maps), an emission map and a specular map. A map multiplies its value (metalness, roughness, emissive); the opacity map replaces the base texture's alpha; occlusion darkens only ambient light, by `occlusion_strength`. A specular map scales how strongly non-metals reflect: plain 0.5 is the usual 4%, 0 none, 1 twice that (older Phong materials' specular maps, rather than a guessed metalness). A built-in shape uses those maps when its material has any.
- **Collision pieces** (R15.2; `assets/src/model/collision.rs`): an object named like another with `_COL` on the end, or `_COL` and a number (`Rock_COL`, `Rock_col2`; the suffix in any case), is one convex piece of that object's collision, and everything inside it belongs to the same piece. Its corners go into `Model::collision` (one list per piece, fitted with the full model's transform); it isn't drawn and doesn't size the model or add to its hull. FBX pieces are read even when hidden. A pick of the object (`file.glb#Rock`) brings its pieces, as it does its LOD copies. Cooked models keep them. A MeshPart whose `CollisionFidelity` is `Default` collides as them ([physics.md](../architecture/physics.md)); this is the same idea as Unreal's `UCX_` and Godot's `-colonly` objects, so they can be made in Blender.
- **Stand-ins** (foliage W4, 2026-11-28; `lod::PROXY`): an object named like another with `_PROXY` on the end (any case; everything inside it belongs to it) is that model's stand-in for far shadows and ray tracing: a few cheap triangles (a generated tree's thick limbs and a crossed pair of big cards per cluster on its crown's shell, cut out as its leaves are; its bark opaque). Its primitives go into `Model::proxy`, fitted with the full model's transform; it isn't drawn as the model, doesn't size it or add to its hull, isn't simplified, and a pick of the object brings it. Cooked models keep it. The renderer doesn't use it yet (the speed work wires it into the far shadow cascade and the ray traced scene).
- **Levels of detail** (graphics Phase 13; `assets/src/model/lod.rs`): each primitive has `lods`, simpler copies with the same material, each with fewer triangles than the one before. A file's own copies win: objects named like the full one with `_LOD1` and `_LOD2` on the end (any case; `_LOD0` is the full one, and everything inside an `_LOD1` node belongs to that copy; FBX copies are read even when hidden, since a LOD group hides all but one), matched to the full primitives by material name (or to the only one); a pick of the full object (`file.glb#Rock`) brings its copies with it. They don't size the model or add to its hull, and are fitted with the full model's transform. Without them, a primitive of 256 triangles or more is simplified with meshoptimizer (`meshopt`) at load to half, then a quarter, of its triangles (normals and texture coordinates weighed so creases and seams stay, the surface moving at most 2% and 5% of its size), keeping only the corners each copy uses; a copy that doesn't save a fifth on the level before is dropped. Cut-out primitives (`AlphaMode::Mask`: leaves, fences) aren't simplified (2026-11-14): moving their corners stretched the texture's holes, so leaves changed shape and bushes visibly thinned or clumped at the switch; a file's own `_LOD1`/`_LOD2` copies of them are still used. **A far copy for plants** (2026-11-28, `model/cards.rs`): a cut-out primitive whose file has both its copies gets a third, its last copy with half its leaf cards (`FAR_KEEP`), kept cards spread evenly (ordered along a Morton curve, kept by bit-reversed place) and each grown about its middle by the square root of the area dropped (at most 1.6 times, never past the plant's box), so a leaf keeps its outline, as SpeedTree's and Unreal's foliage thin cards; it's made at load and kept in the cooked model, and the renderer draws it below 5% of the screen's height (the third of `LOD_SCREEN_SIZES`). A primitive needs 32 cards and a middle card of at most 16 triangles (a fence or a net is one sheet and is left alone). Overgrown Street, `owner-ultra`, 2560 × 1405, three alternating runs a view against origin/main f805d102: +1.0% to +2.5% FPS (spawn 40.6 → 41.6), the prepass 0.27–0.37 ms shorter, 1.2–1.5 M fewer triangles a frame; in the stills 0.3–4% of pixels change, 0.007–0.37% by more than 8 levels (the canopy view's, mostly the far trees' shadow on a near tree). `AXCENITY_NO_FAR_CARDS` leaves it out, for comparing, on a model loaded fresh (the cooked copy keeps what it was cooked with). Nor are primitives with blend shapes (2026-11-25): a copy is made from the rest shape alone and may move the surface by centimetres, so a character's garment drawn from one cut through the skin under it, and where that skin is hidden (`model/hide.rs`) left holes that showed only from far away, when the part was under 30% of the picture's height (seen on the human base: the T-shirt, the trousers and the sweater at three and six times the capture tool's `front` distance, its `far` and `farther` views; the hair lost patches the same way). The human base's body, garments and hairstyles all have blend shapes, so they are drawn at full detail at every distance; a file's own copies of such a primitive are still used. The copies are cached with the model in `Assets`, and a project's model also keeps them on disk (roadmap L2; `model/lod_cache.rs`): `.axcenity/imported/meshes/<file name>-<hash of its path>-<key>.axlod` holds each simplified primitive's copies as lists of its corners, where the key hashes the simplifier's settings and the fitted primitives' corners, normals, texture coordinates and triangles (so each pick of objects from a file has its own file, and a changed model or setting just misses). The next load reads them instead of simplifying (CityPropsCollection.fbx: fitting 68 → 6 ms); a damaged or mismatched file is ignored and simplified again. Each file is stamped with the model file it came from: writing one deletes that model's files from older versions of it, and Build Game ships only the current ones. Any host that loads a project model writes them (failing quietly in a read-only folder). `scene` hands them over through `MeshSource::lod_count` and `MeshSource::lod`, and the renderer picks one (see LOD in [pipeline.md](pipeline.md)). A posed model's copies are posed too: `Model::pose` only stores the joint matrices, and `Posed::primitive(model, index, level)` skins that primitive at that level the first time it's asked for, so a far character poses only its simpler copy.
- **Cooked models** (roadmap L2; `assets/src/model/cooked_model.rs`): a project's model that loaded without warnings is kept whole in `.axcenity/imported/models/<file name>-<hash of its path>-<hash of its pick>.axmodel` (fitted triangles, simpler copies, materials and skeleton), and the next load reads that instead of parsing the file (CityPropsCollection.fbx 143 → 2 ms, Walking.fbx 178 → 19–24 ms; [../benchmarks.md](../benchmarks.md)). Textures are named by where they came from (`ImageOrigin`): a project file, loaded again as `read_texture` does (its cooked copy when current), an embedded map's cooked copy, matched by the embedded bytes' hash, or a map cooked in place inside the model (`InModel`, by its hash; [textures.md](textures.md)). A model with a texture that has none of these (an embedded map not cooked yet) isn't kept. A file is used only while it's stamped with the model file as it is now, and was made by the same loading code: `assets/build.rs` hashes the crate's `src/` and the lockfile into `AXCENITY_ASSETS_SOURCE_HASH`, so changing the importer or a reader crate makes every cooked model stale. Writing one deletes the model's stale ones; Build Game ships the current ones (`cooked_models`). The encoder and decoder name every field of `Model`, `Primitive`, `ModelMaterial` and `Rig`, so adding a field doesn't compile until both handle it.
- **Texture quality** (a graphics setting, [settings.md](settings.md)) leaves out a texture's largest mip levels at upload, and so does a GPU whose textures can't be that big. Past the texture memory budget, the least recently drawn textures lose more; see [shadows.md](shadows.md).
- **Binding:** each texture is uploaded once as plain `Rgba8Unorm` with an sRGB view as well: base color and emission are read through the sRGB view (so samples are linear light), the other maps as plain values. Each distinct set of textures and map settings gets one bind group (group 1: 8 textures, the sampler and a small `MapsUniform` with each map's channel mask, inversions, normal scale and green sign, occlusion strength and which maps are present); maps an item lacks bind the 1×1 white texture. Items are batched by mesh and that set.
- **Tangents:** normal maps need them. `assets` works them out after fitting, for primitives with a normal map, from the triangles' texture coordinates (area-weighted, made perpendicular to each normal; `w` is the side the texture's "up" is on). File tangents are ignored, so seams on maps baked with MikkTSpace can show slightly. Vertices carry them at location 13; the shader turns them by the model matrix and builds the tangent frame per pixel.
- **glTF:** base color, metallic-roughness (roughness in green, metalness in blue), normal (with `scale`), occlusion (red, with `strength`; often the same image as metallic-roughness) and emissive textures. All of a primitive's textures must use the same texture coordinate set as its first one; others are left out. A primitive whose material has no texture keeps its first set (`TEXCOORD_0`) anyway, so the Model Studio reads back the uvs it unwrapped (Phase 58); an FBX mesh's uvs are only read when its material has a texture. Vertex colours (`COLOR_0`, read as linear RGBA) are kept on `Primitive::colors`, through simplified copies and cooked models, and written by `mesh_edit`'s `.glb` writer; a generated tree packs its foliage data there (`R` occlusion, `G` sway, `B` flutter, `A` weathering or the leaf cluster's tint). The renderer doesn't read them yet (foliage W3b).
- **FBX:** what `ufbx` maps to PBR, plus the FBX material's own slots: normal map (or a bump map that looks like a normal map: mostly blue, red and green near half; a grey height map is left out), metalness, roughness (inverted for a glossiness map, and for Lambert/Phong, whose shininess map is glossiness), ambient occlusion, emission (a map with no emission color glows in its own colors), opacity (its own map, or the base texture's alpha when it's the same image; alpha if the map has any, else red; any opacity map cuts out at 0.5) and, for Lambert/Phong, specular. A value with a map takes the map's value alone (factor 1), as FBX programs do.
- **Texture roles by name** (Phase 20, FBX; `model/maps.rs`): every texture a material links is also judged by its file name, the last telling word deciding (`Bench_BaseColor_4K`, `T_Bench_N`, `rock_ORM`; words split at `_ - .`, spaces and lowercase-to-capital, sizes such as `4K` and numbers skipped; one-letter suffixes such as `_N`, `_R`, `_M` only at the end). Channel-packed names are split into their channels: ORM/ARM (occlusion, roughness, metalness in R, G, B), RMA, MRA, metallic-roughness (G, B), Unity's metallic-smoothness (metalness R, smoothness A, inverted), HDRP's mask map (metalness R, occlusion G, smoothness A), specular-smoothness (specular RGB, smoothness A) and albedo-transparency (base color, opacity A, cut out). `_NormalDX` maps flip green (a glTF file's normal texture too, when its file is named so: the Model Studio's DirectX bakes, Phase 61). A packed name always wins over the slot the texture is linked to; for Lambert/Phong, whose few slots exporters misuse (Unity puts metallic-smoothness in specular), any name wins; for PBR materials a single-map name only fills slots the links left empty. A texture linked to a property the engine doesn't read is used for its name's role, or noted as unknown ("has a texture `wood.png` it doesn't know the use of…") and left out; height maps (by name, or linked as bump/displacement and not normal-looking) are noted as unused.
- **Unlinked textures** (FBX): textures no material links, often embedded ones, go to the file's only material, or the material whose name (without words such as `mat` and `shader`) is the longest part of the texture's name, and fill only empty maps.
- **SurfaceAppearance** (Phase 21, `scene`): a SurfaceAppearance inside a Part replaces that part's flat color with its maps (the part's `Color` still tints the color map; a metalness or roughness map uses factor 1). `TileSize` on the Part (studs per repeat, default 8; 0 leaves the coordinates unscaled) scales each face by that edge's world length divided by `TileSize`, so a long part repeats the texture instead of stretching it once. Imported models keep their own coordinates. Inside a MeshPart, a SurfaceAppearance replaces the maps of the model's material named its `MaterialName` (others keep the file's). It decides every map: an empty one is no map, so deleting the SurfaceAppearance is the way back to the part's Color and Material, or the file's own. Its `Color` multiplies the material's color. A metalness or roughness map gives the value alone (factor 1); without one, the material's own value is used. `MetalnessChannel`, `RoughnessChannel`, `AmbientOcclusionChannel` and `OpacityChannel` pick channels (`Enum.TextureChannel`), `InvertRoughness` reads a glossiness map, `InvertOpacity` an opacity map that's white where see-through, `NormalStrength` and `NormalMapDirectX` set the normal map, `AmbientOcclusionStrength` the occlusion, and `EmissiveStrength` multiplies the glow (an emission map on a material that doesn't glow glows in the map's colors). `Tiling` (a Vector2, default 1, 1) repeats its maps that many times as often across and down, and `Offset` (a Vector2, default 0, 0) then shifts them, in copies of the texture; on a Part they apply after `TileSize`, and on a MeshPart they replace the file's own texture transform for that material. An opacity map on a solid material cuts it out at 0.5. Map files are read through the project's `Assets` (`Assets::texture`), decoded once, and a file that can't be loaded is reported once ("Could not load the texture `…`: …") and left out. The editor calls `Assets::load_textures_in_background`: a texture not read yet is then left out (drawn as if it had no map) while up to four worker threads decode it, and each one finished wakes the editor to draw again; `Assets::texture_progress` says how many are done. The player keeps reading them before its first frame, on the loading screen. Debug builds optimize dependencies and `render` (`[profile.dev.package]` in the workspace `Cargo.toml`), since unoptimized JPEG decoding and mipmapping take seconds per 4K map.
