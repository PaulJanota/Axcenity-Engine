# Hardware ray tracing

Optional ray traced effects on GPUs that can trace rays. Every effect is off unless a setting turns it on, and today's renderer is the fallback for each. Why ray queries, which effects come in which order and what they cost: [research/hardware-ray-tracing.md](../research/hardware-ray-tracing.md) (the RT0 to RT5 plan). This page is what's built.

## Which GPUs (RT0)

The renderer uses wgpu's ray queries (`Features::EXPERIMENTAL_RAY_QUERY`): rays traced from ordinary compute and fragment shaders against acceleration structures. They work on DirectX 12 (ray tracing tier 1.1, shader model 6.5), Vulkan (`VK_KHR_ray_query`) and Metal (macOS 15 or later). Ray tracing pipelines, which only Vulkan has in wgpu, aren't used.

- **Opening the device, only when asked.** `render::adapters::with_ray_queries(adapter, descriptor)` adds the feature, the adapter's acceleration structure limits and wgpu's experimental opt-in (`ExperimentalFeatures::enabled`, the workspace's one `unsafe` call outside the script debugger) when the adapter has it and hardware ray tracing is wanted (`adapters::want_ray_tracing`). The player (`Renderer::new`), the editor (`wgpu_options`), the benchmarks and the ray tracing tests all open their device through it. A device opened with ray queries behaves differently even while nothing traces rays: on an RX 9070 XT under DirectX 12 a white square flashed in the editor's viewport now and then, and didn't with the device opened without them. So it's off until `GraphicsSettings::ray_tracing` (Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing > **Hardware ray tracing**, `graphics.json`'s `ray_tracing`) turns it on, and it changes at the next start: the player hands the saved setting to `want_ray_tracing` before opening its window, and the editor keeps it in `hardware-ray-tracing.txt` beside eframe's settings (its device exists before its settings are read). `adapters::adapter_can_ray_trace` says whether the adapter could, so Settings can say what a restart would do. `AXCENITY_RAY_TRACING=1` opens devices with it whatever the setting, and `=0` without it.
- **Capability.** `Capabilities::ray_queries` says whether the device was opened with it (`read_capabilities`), and `EffectiveGraphics::ray_queries` carries it to the renderer. `TextureRenderer::limit_capabilities` can turn it off for fallback tests.
- **Settings.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_tracing_distance` (an override; `graphics.json`'s `ray_tracing_distance`, in studs, 50 to 5,000) is how far from the camera items are put in the ray traced scene. Presets: Low 150, Medium 250, High 400, Ultra 800. It's the machine's choice, set in the editor's Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing or `graphics.json`, not from scripts (like the texture memory budget). Hardware ray tracing is the machine's too, but a game can ask for it (RL2 of [runtime-lighting-parity.md](../research/runtime-lighting-parity.md)): `UserGameSettings.HardwareRayTracing` saves it for the next start (`RayTracingSupported` and `RayTracingActive` say whether the GPU could and whether it is), and a project's `ray_traced_by_default` gives a game's first start the one-click look on a GPU that ray traces ([../project/export.md](../project/export.md)). The player's F9 Stats tab has a Ray tracing line while anything is traced. Each ray traced effect adds its own switch and quality there, off at every preset.

## The ray traced scene (RT0)

`renderer/ray_tracing.rs`'s `RayScene` is made only while something traces rays on a device that can (`ScenePipeline::traces_rays`: ray traced shadows or ambient occlusion, or the editor's Ray Hits or Path Traced view), so a frame without ray tracing records nothing for it. A frame that traces nothing while a ray traced effect is still on in the settings (the world's shadows off, no light probe volume, water gone from the list; `ScenePipeline::may_trace_rays`) parks the scene, BLASes and all (`parked_ray_scene`), and the next frame that traces takes it back, so nothing is built again: Desert Homestead's first frame builds 1,495 meshes in about 4 s. It's freed once every ray traced effect is off (or the device can't trace), and a parked scene drops the BLAS of any mesh that leaves the GPU (`undrawn.rs`, the memory budgets).

- **One BLAS per mesh** (bottom-level acceleration structure), keyed by `MeshKey`, built from the mesh's own vertex and index buffers (their position comes first in each corner; `BufferUsages::BLAS_INPUT` is added to mesh buffers on devices with ray queries). New meshes are built up to `BLAS_TRIANGLES_PER_FRAME` (2 million triangles, about 5 ms on an RX 9070 XT) a frame, so a big import builds over a few frames; one mesh bigger than that is built whole on a frame of its own. Its items are left out until it's built (`RayTracingStats::meshes_waiting`). A caller's mesh whose `MeshSource::version` changes is built again: wgpu has no refit, so every update is a full build. A BLAS unused for 600 frames is freed.
- **One TLAS** (top-level structure) with an instance per item, in draw-list order, holding the item's transform, its BLAS and a mask: solid (1), cut-out (2), see-through or water (4), or not casting shadows (8). It's rebuilt only when an instance changes (an item moved, came, went or its mesh was rebuilt); wgpu uploads instances from the lowest changed one up, so a still scene costs nothing after its first frame.
- **A slot per item**: the row rays look their hit's item up in (each effect's table: cut-outs, surfaces, the path tracer's normals). An instance's custom data is its first slot, and a hit's slot is that plus the geometry it hit (`hit.instance_custom_data + hit.geometry_index` in every shader that reads one). An item with an instance of its own has one geometry, so its slot is its instance's; swaying plants bent by the wind share an instance, a geometry and a slot each (next). Slots with an instance of their own come first, then each shared instance's together.
- **Swaying plants** (`renderer/ray_sway.rs`, 2026-11-14; bent together since 2026-11-25): while the wind blows, an item that sways (`DrawItem::sway`) gets a bent copy of its mesh each frame: `ray_sway.wgsl` bends every corner with the same `swayed_at` (sway.wgsl) the scene and shadow passes use, so the TLAS is rebuilt every frame then. With no wind (or a vertical one) nothing is bent and every item uses its mesh's shared BLAS. Before, rays saw every plant at rest, so ray traced leaf shadows stood still while the leaves moved, and the swayed surface could shade itself. `RayTracingStats::bent` counts them.
  - **Which**: the nearest first, up to `BENT_TRIANGLES` (400,000 triangles a frame); the rest keep their mesh's rest pose. An item the wind moves less than a pixel on screen (`SEEN_SWAY_PIXELS`: `Sway::reach_in` the wind's speed, at its distance, through `Sight`) is traced at rest too: its shadow lands on the same pixels either way, and such items are most of a wide view's.
  - **Bent together.** What costs is each BLAS, each dispatch and each buffer written, not the triangles: about 3 µs of GPU time and 5 µs of the frame's thread a plant when each had a BLAS, a buffer and a dispatch of its own, which was 13 ms and 20 ms a frame for a meadow's 4,100 clumps ([performance.md](performance.md), "Swaying plants bent together"). So every copy is bent into one positions buffer (`BentMeshes::positions`), where it stands in the world, by one dispatch a mesh (a run: `RunRaw` at a dynamic offset in one uniform buffer, each item's matrix and sway in one storage buffer, written only when the items change).
  - **Bundles.** The copies are built as the geometries of a few BLASes (`PREFER_FAST_BUILD`), each with one TLAS instance that moves nothing. A bundle (`BundleKey`) holds what one instance can: cut-outs or not (a geometry flag), one mask, one index width, and one square of the ground (`bundle_cell`: as wide as its items are far from the camera, to the power of two below, 8 studs at least, fixed in the world), so a view has a hundred or two, each small enough that rays elsewhere pass it by (one BLAS for everything traced a quarter slower). A bundle's geometries go biggest mesh first, and its BLAS is made with room to spare (`room_for`): wgpu lets a BLAS be built with fewer and smaller geometries than it was made for, so as the camera moves and the copies change it is seldom made again. Bundles, and each mesh's bind group, are freed after 120 frames unused.
- **Left out:** items whose bounding sphere is past the ray tracing distance, and the copy fading out of a level-of-detail switch. A painted plant past its shadow's reach is traced but not by shadow rays (its instance mask), and over the band before that its shadow thins out ray by ray ([shadows.md](shadows.md)). Items in groups (painted plants, `DrawList::chunks`) are also passed over when no view of the frame reaches them (`Reach`, `LodStates::resolve`): the camera's, the shadow maps' cascades and the lamps' shadow views, so a frame with ray traced effects on traced 14,900 of Desert Homestead's 42,000 items in reach. A plant beside the screen can still stand between the sun and what the camera sees, so while ray traced shadows are on and the shadow maps reach less far than the rays (or aren't drawn), the frame also keeps what the sun's view of the camera's view holds (`sun_reach` in `renderer/lighting.rs`, 2026-11-26: before it such a plant's shadow was missing until the plant was turned into view). Ray traced ambient occlusion, GI and reflections still see only what the frame's views reach. `RayTracingStats::bent_left_out` counts swaying items traced at rest for the bending budget. Cut-outs let rays through their holes (below).
- **Posed characters** (skinned or morphed items, RL1 of [runtime-lighting-parity.md](../research/runtime-lighting-parity.md), 2026-11-14; built from the posed buffer since CO8, 2026-11-17): a BLAS of the mesh would be its rest pose, so each frame a posed item within reach gets a BLAS of its own (`PREFER_FAST_BUILD`), built straight from the frame's posed corners (`renderer/posing.rs`: the item's joints, identity shapes and expressions, exactly as every raster pass draws it; positions are the first three words of each 20-byte corner), which its TLAS slot uses, like a swaying plant's bent copy (both are `BentMeshes` in `renderer/ray_sway.rs`). Nearest first, up to `POSED_TRIANGLES` (300,000 a frame, a budget apart from the plants' so a windy forest never takes the players' shadows); a character past it is left out of the ray traced scene for that frame (its rest pose would cast a T-shaped shadow), counted in `RayTracingStats::posed_left_out` (`posed` counts the ones traced). A posed item is in reach by its posed bounds (`GpuSkin::bounds`, `ray_tracing::within_reach`); one no view draws but within reach is posed for the rays alone. So characters cast ray traced shadows of their own shape, darken ray traced AO and block ray traced GI and water reflections. Before CO8 the copy was skinned without its blend shapes, so a live character (ADR-0041) shadowed itself with the base body's shape. The software scene (`ray_soft.rs`) still leaves skinned items out.
- **Still groups** (`renderer/ray_groups.rs`, 2026-11-27): small still items share a BLAS by the square of ground they stand on, one TLAS instance a group, so a meadow isn't a hundred thousand instances. An item of at most `GROUPED_TRIANGLES` (256) triangles that isn't posed, isn't swaying where its sway shows and isn't see-through goes in the group of its `GroupKey` (cut-out or not, mask, index width and `bundle_cell`, as a bundle's), once the square has `MIN_GROUP` (4) such items. Each item is a geometry of the group's BLAS: its own mesh's vertex and index buffers placed by its transform (`BlasTriangleGeometry::transform_buffer`, the BLAS made with `USE_TRANSFORM` and `PREFER_FAST_TRACE`), so a group holds its own copy of its items' triangles and the trees (most of a forest's triangles) keep their instances. A hit's slot is the group's first plus the geometry, as for a bundle, so every slot table reads the same, and the slots of each group's items come after the items with instances of their own and before the bundles (which change every frame; wgpu uploads instances from the first that changed). A group is built again only when its items change (its fingerprint: each item's mesh and version, cut-out and transform, in order, hashed on the renderer's threads), up to `GROUP_TRIANGLES_PER_FRAME` (1 million triangles, about 2.6 ms of GPU) a frame, nearest squares first; a group waiting for the budget traces its items as instances of their own that frame, so the picture never waits. Groups unused for 120 frames are freed. In Overgrown Street (113,000 traced items, 1996 × 703, the owner's settings with ray tracing) the TLAS went from 113,000 instances to about 20,000: wgpu's submit 6.3 → 1.3 ms and encoding 3.8 → 2.3 ms of the frame's thread, and the ray scene's own work 2.0 ms less; the bigger BLASes cost the GPU about 0.9 ms of ray traced shadows and 0.4 of ambient occlusion. Grouped or not, the stills are the same (at most 2 levels of 255 on a few pixels). `RayTracingStats::groups`, `grouped` and `groups_built` count them. `AXCENITY_NO_RAY_GROUPS=1` traces every item as an instance of its own, to compare.
- **Distances** (2026-11-27, `ray_tracing.rs`): swaying items further than `SWAY_DISTANCE` (32 studs, to the nearest point of their bounds) are traced at rest, and their items aren't read for their sway (in Overgrown Street the bending budget ran out at about 26 studs anyway, and deciding each further plant's sway read 48,000 items a frame: 7.5 ms of the frame's thread with the submit of the bundles); the item loop that finds what's within the ray tracing distance runs on the renderer's threads. A small plant (a cut-out whose bounds' radius is at most `SMALL_PLANT_RADIUS`, 4 studs) casts its ray traced shadow to `SMALL_PLANT_SHADOW_REACH` (55 studs from the camera), thinning out over the last `SMALL_PLANT_SHADOW_FADE` (5) by `CutOutRaw::casting`, and past it isn't in the shadow rays' mask. Rays pass through a cut-out's holes only within `HOLES_REACH` (60 studs): further off its slot has no holes (`CutOutRaw` without a layer), so every hit on its cards counts and a shadow ray ends at the first leaf it meets; its BLAS stays a cut-out's, so `cut_out_near` still keeps a leaf from shading itself, and a thinning shadow still thins ray by ray. The owner chose those distances (2026-11-27). Measured in Overgrown Street: the hole distance saves 2.4 ms of the GPU (ray traced shadows 1.8, ambient occlusion 0.4) and 3.1 ms of the frame's thread (no hole table looked up for far cut-outs), and darkens what's past it, most of all in wide views over the canopy (a view from 30 studs up: mean −2.2 levels, 5.2% of pixels by more than 8, at most 28; at eye height −0.5 to −0.6, 0.6%), with a visible edge where it starts; the small plants' distance measured nothing either way in those views. `AXCENITY_NO_SWAY_DISTANCE`, `AXCENITY_NO_SMALL_PLANT_REACH` and `AXCENITY_NO_HOLES_REACH` lift one of them, `AXCENITY_NO_RAY_REACHES` all three.
- **Stand-ins** (2026-11-28): an item the camera draws at `STAND_IN_LEVEL` (2) or a simpler copy is traced with its model's stand-in (`MeshSource::stand_in`, a `_PROXY` object, [models.md](models.md): a generated tree's thick limbs and a few big crossed leaf cards, about 1,200 triangles where its full detail has many thousand) in place of the copy drawn, once the stand-in is on the GPU, with the item's material (a cut-out's holes too); a piece the stand-in covers (a tree's trunk base) is left out once it is (`StandInKey::mesh`). The ray traced scene finds each grouped item's stand-in in its cluster (`Cluster::stand_ins`). The stand-in carries the tree's per-corner foliage data, so its bent copy sways as the tree does. Only models with a `_PROXY` object change; `AXCENITY_NO_STAND_INS=1` traces the meshes drawn instead, for comparing.
- **Cut-outs' holes** (`renderer/ray_cut_outs.rs`): a mesh drawn by a cut-out item (`alpha_cutoff`, solid otherwise) gets a BLAS of its own built without the opaque flag (`BlasKey` is the mesh and whether it's for a cut-out; one drawn both ways has both). When it's built, `ray_cut_out_uvs.wgsl` copies its texture coordinates, three a triangle, into one shared buffer (so mesh buffers are storage-readable on devices with ray queries), and the base color texture's alpha is shrunk on the CPU to 512 × 512 (`ALPHA_SIZE`, the mean of the pixels each texel covers) into a layer of an `R8Unorm` array, once per texture. Each TLAS slot gets a `CutOutRaw` (the mesh's first triangle, the layer, the cutoff and the material's texture coordinate turn, scale and offset). A query that doesn't force opacity sees a cut-out's triangles as candidates, and `cut_out_covers` confirms a hit only where the alpha at its texture coordinates reaches the cutoff. Items tiled by size (`uv_tile`) and cut-outs without a texture are traced as solid. Ray traced shadows test the holes (their own copy in ray_shadows.wgsl), and so do ray traced ambient occlusion and GI (LE7) through `ray_cut_outs.wgsl`'s `trace_through_holes`, which runs a whole query on the including shader's `ray_scene` (naga takes no pointer to a ray query, so a query can't be handed to a helper); the Ray Hits view still forces opacity (cut-outs as solid cards). Ray traced GI's compute pass binds only the frame bindings it reads (`ray_gi::FRAME_BINDINGS`, through `ScenePipeline::frame_subset`): with all five of the frame's storage buffers, its own and the cut-outs' would pass wgpu's default eight a stage.
- **Timing:** the builds are recorded first in the frame, as `FramePass::AccelerationStructures` ("Acceleration structures" in the profiler), `None` when nothing was built. `RenderStats::ray_tracing` (`RayTracingStats`) counts the traced items, the built meshes and their triangles, the meshes waiting and whether the TLAS was rebuilt; all zero while nothing traces rays.

## Ray Hits view (RT0)

Viewport > View Mode > **Ray Hits** (`DebugView::RayHits`, `ray_hits.wgsl`) traces one ray per pixel from the camera through the ray traced scene: each item a color of its own (from its TLAS slot), its triangles alternately lighter and darker, dimmer toward the ray tracing distance, and a dark blue-grey where a ray hits nothing. It shows what ray traced effects will see, so an item missing from it (past the distance, a character past the posing budget, or a mesh still waiting) won't cast ray traced shadows either. Its legend counts the scene, or says the GPU can't ray trace; on such a GPU the view is plain dark.

## Path Traced view (LE6)

Viewport > View Mode > **Path Traced** (`DebugView::PathTraced`, `renderer/path_tracer.rs`, `path_tracer.wgsl`) is a reference picture: what the scene's light would look like followed ray by ray, to compare the lit view's shortcuts against. It's a debug view, so it exists only while shown (made in `set_debug_view`, as it reads the frame's bindings) and only on a GPU with ray queries; elsewhere it's plain dark like Ray Hits.

- **Each frame** a compute pass adds one path a pixel to a sum (a `vec4` a pixel: light and count), and a fullscreen pass (`path_traced_view.wgsl`) shows the average through the classic tone mapping curve over the finished frame. A path starts at the camera a little off the pixel's middle each frame (so edges smooth out), and at each hit adds the item's glow, the sun (a shadow ray toward a random point of its disc, as wide as the shadows' softness, so soft shadows come out right) and every lamp in reach (each with its own shadow ray, whatever its `Shadows`), then bounces on in a cosine-weighted direction carrying the item's color, for up to 4 bounces; after 2 it may stop, the likelier the darker it's become (Russian roulette). A path that leaves the scene sees the sky: as drawn for the camera's own rays, and times the environment intensity (without the sun's disc) once bounced. A path brighter than 64 is dimmed to it, so one lucky bounce doesn't leave a speck.
- **Starts again** when the camera moves, the output's size changes, the TLAS is rebuilt (an item moved, came or went) or the light changes (`path_tracer::lighting_key`: the sun, the sky and every lamp's bytes); after `MOST_PATHS` (2,048) a pixel the picture stays as it is and nothing is traced. A picture too big for one storage buffer is traced at half the size (or less) and shown stretched. The editor's legend counts the paths so far (`RayTracingStats::path_traced_paths`).
- **Normals**: rays don't give a hit's normal, so each traced mesh's are copied once on the GPU (`path_tracer_normals.wgsl`, three a triangle packed as 16-bit pairs, like cut-outs' texture coordinates) and each TLAS slot gets its mesh's first triangle; a hit's normal is its corners' blended by where it was hit, turned into the world by its item's world-to-object matrix (normals turn by the inverse transpose; the matrix is in the slot's row, `SlotRaw`, since bent plants share an instance that isn't turned), and flipped to face the ray.
- **What it reads**: the TLAS, each slot's `SurfaceRaw` (color times the texture's average, and glow: the ray traced scene keeps them while the view is shown, as for ray traced GI), cut-outs' holes (`ray_cut_outs.wgsl`, for paths and shadows alike), and of the frame's bindings only the uniform, the lamps and the sky (`FRAME_BINDINGS`), so the pass stays within the default eight storage buffers a stage.
- **Left out**: every surface is matte (no gloss, metal or texture detail beyond its average color); see-through items and water aren't hit; fog, clouds' shadows and the flat ambient light aren't added; anything past the ray tracing distance isn't in the scene (as for every ray traced effect).

## Is it on? The viewport badge

While Hardware ray tracing is on (or turned on, waiting for a restart), the editor's viewport shows a small badge at the bottom right (`viewport/ray_badge.rs`), so whether rays are drawing is never a guess:

- **Ray tracing: restart to turn on** (warning color), with a **Restart now** button: the setting is on but the GPU was opened without it. Restart now starts the editor again with the project and its unsaved world and script edits (`docs/editor/scripts.md`, Restart now); Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing has the same button while a restart would turn ray tracing on or off.
- **Ray tracing: kept off by AXCENITY_RAY_TRACING=0** (warning color), with **Restart now**: the setting is on, but the editor was started with the override (`adapters::ray_tracing_override`). Restart now starts it without the variable (`relaunch::restart` removes `RAY_TRACING_VARIABLE` from the new process), since a restart is for the settings to apply.
- **Ray tracing: not on this GPU** (warning color): the GPU or driver can't ray trace.
- **Ray tracing on: no effects**: the GPU ray traces, but no ray traced effect is turned on, so nothing looks different yet.
- **Ray tracing: Shadows High, AO Medium · 812 items traced**: the effects drawing and how many items are in the ray traced scene this frame ("nothing in reach yet" when none).

While effects draw, the badge's **Compare** toggle draws the view without them (`Viewport::rays_hidden`, `GraphicsSettings::without_ray_traced_effects` for the viewport only; the badge then says "Ray tracing: hidden to compare"), so what they add can be seen side by side without a restart or a settings change. Hovering it gives the reach, the scene's meshes and triangles, any meshes still waiting to be built, and where to change it. With the setting off the badge is hidden, and **Viewport > Ray Tracing Badge** (also in Settings > Viewport and Ctrl+K; saved between runs) hides it whenever wanted, ending a Compare so nothing stays hidden.

## Ray tracing section in Settings

Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing is organized into two cards: **Hardware acceleration** (status pill, the **Hardware ray tracing** startup toggle with **Restart now** when a restart is needed, and **Ray tracing distance**) and **Effects** (Shadows, Ambient occlusion, Reflections, and Global illumination, with inline **Needs Hardware RT** / **Hardware RT** / **Software fallback** indicators and a **Turn all off** button (`without_ray_traced_effects`) that clears every ray traced effect override without a restart). `GraphicsSettings::ray_traced` remains available for games' `ray_traced_by_default`. The **In use** section below shows a structured card with summary status pills, a grouped key-value breakdown (Core & resolution, Lighting & shadows, Ray tracing, Atmosphere & post-processing) with custom-override markers, and GPU limitation callouts. While the setting is on but the GPU was opened without ray queries on a card that offers them, the section offers Restart now. The Learn guide `documentation/learn/ray-tracing.md` walks through ray tracing, the badge, and getting good shadows and sky.

## Ray traced sun shadows (RT1)

The sun's shadows traced as rays from each pixel instead of read from the shadow map cascades: exact up close, with no cascade blur, acne or peter-panning, and soft by how far the caster is from the receiver.

- **Setting.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_traced_shadows` (`RayTracedShadows`: Off, Low, Medium, High; `graphics.json`'s `ray_traced_shadows`) is Off at every preset and Off without ray queries, that is while Hardware ray tracing is off or the GPU can't (`effective` adds the note "Ray traced shadows are off: Hardware ray tracing is off or this GPU can't ray trace…"). How soft shadows look stays the world's choice (`Lighting.ShadowSoftness`, ADR-0013); the setting only picks the rays a pixel where shadows are soft: Low 1, Medium 2, High 4. Hard shadows (softness 0) always trace one. **Resolution** (2026-11-27, `GraphicsSettings::ray_traced_shadow_resolution`, `RayShadowResolution`, `graphics.json`'s `ray_traced_shadow_resolution`): Full traces from every pixel, Half from one pixel of each 2 × 2 (a different one each frame under TAA, which gathers all four; one diagonal, then the other) and fills the other three (`cs_fill`, below); Half at every preset since 2026-11-28 (until then Full at Ultra): in Overgrown Street at Ultra, 2560 × 1405, Full cost 13–41% of the frame rate (spawn 19.0 → 25.0 frames a second with Half, houseA 20.4 → 27.8, canopy 16.9 → 23.9, high 20.0 → 22.7), and Desert Homestead's sunlit frames, moving (a walk, a turn, a still camera in the wind, 48 frames each), differ by more than 8 levels in 0.5–1% of their pixels (shadow edges a fraction of a pixel softer, thin lines at silhouettes) with no more change from frame to frame (−0.9%, −0.5%, +1.3%). Full is an override. It's beside the shadows' setting in Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing (enabled while they're on). It's in the editor's Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing, `UserGameSettings.RayTracedShadows` (`Enum.RayTracedShadows`) for LocalScripts and the blocks' "set graphics *RayTracedShadows* to".
- **When.** `renderer/ray_shadows.rs`'s `RayShadowPlan::of` traces them while the setting is on, the GPU can ray trace and the world's shadows are on (`ShadowDistance` above 0). They reach the ray tracing distance or the world's `ShadowDistance`, whichever is nearer. The prepass is drawn for them (`targets_used`).
- **The pass** (`ray_shadows.wgsl`, `FramePass::RayTracedShadows`, "Ray traced shadows" in the profiler, after ambient occlusion): `cs_trace` rebuilds each prepass pixel's world point and normal and traces toward the sun through solid items and cut-outs (instance masks 1 and 2; see-through items and items with `CastShadow` off aren't hit), starting a little off the surface by distance. A surface facing away from the sun is in its own shadow without a ray. Where the sun is ray traced, contact shadows are left out (`sun_arrival`): rays already find what they look for, and their fixed per-pixel steps only added a speckle. Soft shadows spread their rays over the sun's disc (`MAX_SUN_SIZE` × softness wide) in a golden-angle spiral turned per pixel, and per frame under TAA, and keep the mean distance to what blocked them. `cs_blur_across` and `cs_blur_down` then smooth that across and down by each pixel's soft edge (blocker distance × sun size, in pixels, at least 2 (`MIN_BLUR`: under grass every thin blade's shadow is all soft edge, and 4 rays alone left a fixed speckle, 2026-11-14) and up to 12 each way), leaving out neighbours off the pixel's surface (farther from its plane than 1% of the distance plus 0.05 studs; comparing distances from the camera instead left slopes seen side-on unsmoothed, a dither) or facing another way. Hard shadows skip both. **At half resolution** `cs_trace` runs one thread a 2 × 2 block, on its pixel for the frame (`traced_in_block`, `RayShadows::size.zw`), writing the soft passes' format even for hard shadows; `cs_fill` then gives each other pixel what the traced pixels of the 3 × 3 blocks around it saw, weighted by a Gaussian of their distance (`FILL_SPREAD`, 1.5 pixels) and kept to its surface as the smoothing is (its plane within 1% of the distance plus 0.05 studs, and facing the same way); a pixel with none on its surface (a thin blade, a corner) takes the one nearest its plane, and a lamp's bits are the most weighted neighbour's. For soft shadows the smoothing then runs on the filled buffer (rays, filled, across and down by way of the first buffer); hard ones go from the fill to the mask. The fill binds the prepass, the uniform, the two buffers and the lamps' mask, never the TLAS (`fill_entries`). Test: `half_resolution_traces_a_quarter_of_the_pixels_and_shades_like_full` (a hard and a soft shadow's row within three pixels of every pixel's, the mean within 4 levels) and `point_spot_and_rect_lights_cast_ray_traced_shadows_without_shadow_maps` at both resolutions.
- **The mask** is a `u32` per pixel (frame binding 17, a storage buffer since the item shader already reads 16 textures): the surface's distance from the camera in the top 24 bits and how lit it is in the low 8. `lighting.wgsl`'s `sun_visibility` uses it only where the pixel's own distance matches (so see-through surfaces, water and anything the prepass didn't draw keep the cascades) and blends into the cascades from 85% of the reach to the reach. `Frame::ray_shadows` is (on, width, reach, unused). The shadow maps are still drawn: they cover past the reach and see-through surfaces.
- **Cut-outs** shade only where their texture isn't cut out (`cut_out_covers`, above) (since LE7 ray traced ambient occlusion and GI let rays through the holes too, through the shared `ray_cut_outs.wgsl` and its four bindings, `ray_cut_outs::layout_entries`): a tree's leaves cast leaf-shaped shadows with light between them, where a solid card left one blob. A cut-out nearer to the surface than `cut_out_near` still isn't counted, for shadows and ray traced ambient occlusion alike: 0.03 studs plus 0.005 for each stud from the camera, at most `CUT_OUT_NEAR` (0.35, reached at about 64 studs). Up close a fern or grass clump shades the ground at its foot (with a fixed 0.35 the ground right under one stayed lit, 2026-11-14). From a cut-out's own pixel (the prepass marks it: its normal's w is `PREPASS_CUT_OUT`, 0.875) it stays 0.35 at any distance, since a blade's own card (its normals often bent toward the plant's outside) and the cards crossing it at its foot speckled blades once the gap shrank; further away depth is coarser and a card past the bent budget is traced at rest while drawn swaying, so its own card could graze the ray's start (masks: solid 1, cut-out 2, see-through 4, not casting 8 solid and 16 cut-out).
- **Lamps (LE1).** Point, spot and rect lights whose `Shadows` is on are traced in the same pass (`traced_lamps` in ray_shadows.wgsl), with no shadow map needed and no limit by shadow quality: every pixel within the reach traces the first 8 lamps casting shadows that its light tile lists (`TRACED_LAMPS`), each ray from the surface to the light stopping short of it by how far the light's own part reaches (`LightRaw::shadow.z` is 1 plus that, 0 for a lamp casting none; `ray_shadows::traced_start`), so the fixture a lamp sits in doesn't shade it. With soft shadows (`ShadowSoftness` above 0) a point or spot light's rays spread over a bulb `ShadowSoftness` × `MAX_LAMP_SIZE` (1 stud) wide, and a rect light's over its whole rectangle (true area shadows); hard ones trace one ray to the light (a rect light's nearest point). Soft lamps trace the setting's rays each (1, 2 or 4) and aren't smoothed: TAA averages their per-frame turned samples. Results go in the mask's second half (`Frame::ray_shadows.w` is where it starts, a 256-byte-aligned offset in the same buffer, so the frame keeps one binding), 4 bits a lamp; `lamp_arrival` reads its lamp's by counting the tile's shadow-casting lamps before it (`lamp_slots`), and uses the shadow maps (and contact shadows) where the mask has nothing, past the 8th lamp, and fading in toward the reach, as the sun does. The editor's Insert and the Lighting tool now make lights with `Shadows` on (the classes' default stays off, so saved worlds look the same): turn `Shadows` on for existing lamps to give them ray traced shadows.
- **Cascades left out (2026-11-27).** While sun shadows are traced, the sun's nearest cascades whose whole slice of the view, to its far corners, lies within 85% of the traced reach (`lighting::cascades_under_rays`; where `sun_visibility` uses the traced shadow whole) aren't drawn: their views cover nothing (every point lands past their depth, which every lookup skips), the first cascade drawn takes their slices from the camera on, and the frame doesn't keep items for them (`lighting::cascade_views`: a frustum made of a view that covers nothing takes in the whole world, which for a while made the frame resolve every item of Overgrown Street). The last cascade is always drawn. What still reads the maps there (light through a leaf, see-through surfaces, the edges the traced mask leaves out, the sky over a reflection) reads the next cascade, a coarser one. At Ultra (300 studs, cascades ending about 22, 51, 110 and 300 along the view) with a 260-stud reach, a 2.84:1 view at 70° leaves out two cascades and a 16:9 one three; in Overgrown Street that took the Shadows pass from 11.3 to 5.3 ms with no change to the stills. `AXCENITY_NO_CASCADE_SKIP=1` draws them all.
- **Shafts in the air (LE3).** While volumetric fog is drawn ([fog.md](fog.md)), the same pass also traces the air: `cs_air` gives each cell of the fog's 160 × 90 × 64 grid within the reach 4 rays toward the sun, from the first 4 of the points `cell_sun_visibility` uses, shifted each frame by the fog's turn (`air_shift`, a copy of `cell_shift`; the turn is `RayShadowUniform::lamp[2]`) so the fog's history averages rays from all through each cell, and writes how many got through (0 to 65535, `u32::MAX` past the reach) to the mask's third part (`RayShadows::air_words`, 256-byte aligned after the lamps', 3.7 MB). `volumetric_fog.wgsl` reads it where the fog's `dust_count.y` says (0: not traced) and fades into the cascades from 85% of the reach on, as surfaces do. So shafts come through windows (glass is see-through and isn't traced, as it casts no shadow), between leaves (cut-outs' holes) and through gaps with no shadow-map bias or blur, and with shadow quality Off too. Hard edges only (the sun's size isn't sampled); lamps' light in the air still uses their shadow maps.
- **Water reflections (LE5)** are traced where the screen's reflection finds nothing, with hardware ray tracing on, Ray traced reflections on and reflection quality High or Ultra: [water.md](water.md).
- **One query a ray (2026-11-25).** A ray asks once for both kinds of caster (`first_blocker`, masks 1 and 2 together): a solid item's triangles are committed as they come, since its BLAS is opaque, and a cut-out's come as candidates that the shader confirms only past `cut_out_near` and where the texture isn't cut out. Before, every ray was two queries, the solid items' and then the cut-outs' (each had its own nearest distance), so a ray that reached the sun walked the scene twice. A soft shadow's edge is as wide as its blocker is far, and where a solid item and a cut-out both block a ray that distance is the solid item's, as it was: only a ray a cut-out stopped asks a second time, for the solid one (`blocker_distance`), and since 2026-11-28 only the first such ray of a pixel (`solid_here`): the pixel's rays go to points of the sun's disc a fraction of a degree apart, so one answer serves them all, and under a canopy, where leaves stop nearly every ray, each ray asking again walked the scene twice. `AXCENITY_SOLID_EVERY_RAY=1` asks on every ray again, for comparing: in Overgrown Street (`owner-rt-all`, 2560 × 1405) the traced shadows took 25.9–53.6 ms that way and 9.8–34.7 ms asking once, the canopy view the most (53.6 → 34.7). Lamps', hard shadows' and the air's rays only ask whether anything blocks them (`is_blocked`).
- **What binds the scene.** wgpu checks every instance of a TLAS for each pass that binds it, at submit (`validate_acceleration_structure_actions`, `set_acceleration_structure_dependencies`): with 23,000 instances that is about 0.3 ms a pass on the frame's thread. So `cs_trace` and `cs_air` share one compute pass and one binding of the scene, and the two smoothing passes have a layout of their own without it (`smoothing_entries`; `only_the_passes_that_trace_bind_the_ray_traced_scene`). Three passes a frame bind the scene now (these rays, ambient occlusion's, reflections') where six did.
- **Cost.** One ray a pixel for hard shadows; Low, Medium and High trace 1, 2 and 4 for soft ones, plus two smoothing passes. On the RX 9070 XT, Desert Homestead at 2560 × 1405 with the GPU as the limit (2026-11-25, [performance.md](performance.md#encoding-on-several-threads-and-one-query-a-ray-the-audits-third-round-desert-homestead)): the sun's rays 3.1 ms, the air's 1.1 ms, the smoothing 0.45 ms.

## Ray traced ambient occlusion (RT3)

Corners and creases darkened by short rays instead of guessed from the screen: an occluder off screen, or behind something else, darkens too, and nothing in front darkens what's behind it (screen-space ambient occlusion's halos).

- **Setting.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_traced_ambient_occlusion` (`RayTracedAmbientOcclusion`: Off, Low, Medium, High; `graphics.json`'s `ray_traced_ambient_occlusion`) is Off at every preset and Off without ray queries, with a note. Low traces 2 rays a pixel, Medium and High 4. High traced 8 until 2026-11-28: with TAA, which turns each pixel's rays every frame and gathers them, 4 drew the same picture in Overgrown Street (a walk, a turn under the canopy, a still camera in the wind), Desert Homestead (a walk, a turn at the porch, the windmill in the wind; and the tour's six stills) and the Showcase gallery interior (a walk, a turn, a still camera): at most 0.001% of pixels more than 8 levels of 255 apart (the most by 24), the same mean brightness and the same change from frame to frame, for half the pass. In Overgrown Street (`owner-rt-all`, 2560 × 1405) that was AO 11.5 → 5.7 ms at the spawn, 14.6 → 7.3 under the canopy, and 25.3 → 30.1, 28.4 → 32.3, 24.1 → 29.5 and 23.1 → 26.1 frames a second in the four views. How far rays look (`OCCLUSION_RADIUS`, 1.5 studs) and how dark it gets (`Lighting.AmbientOcclusion`, the world's strength) are the same as screen-space's, so turning it on changes accuracy, not the look (ADR-0013). On, ambient occlusion is drawn even while the screen-space setting is Off (`lighting::occlusion_drawn`, `targets_used`). It's in the editor's Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing, `UserGameSettings.RayTracedAmbientOcclusion` (`Enum.RayTracedAmbientOcclusion`) and the blocks' "set graphics *RayTracedAmbientOcclusion* to".
- **The pass** (`renderer/ray_ao.rs`, `ray_ao.wgsl`; timed inside `FramePass::AmbientOcclusion`) is a fragment pass at half the scene's size into ambient occlusion's own raw target, through its bindings (the prepass's depth and normals, `OcclusionUniform`) and its own (the TLAS, `RayAoUniform`). Each pixel traces cosine-weighted rays over the hemisphere above its surface (a golden-angle spiral turned per pixel, and per frame under TAA) against solid items, cut-outs and items not casting shadows (masks 1, 2, 8 and 16: what the prepass draws), taking the nearest hit (`nearest_hit`): since 2026-11-28 two queries, the solid items' first (their BLASes are opaque, so it never comes back to the shader) and then the cut-outs' only between `cut_out_near` and that solid hit, confirming candidates where the texture isn't cut out. A candidate nearer than `cut_out_near`, or past what's solid, is then never handed back at all; asked together in one query (2026-11-25 to 2026-11-28) every one of them was. Rays against solid items alone take 2.5–3.2 ms of Overgrown Street's AO and the cut-outs the rest (their holes' texture 2–3 ms, handing back their candidates most of it); two queries made AO 0.4–1.1 ms shorter there (`owner-rt-all`, 2560 × 1405: spawn 12.25 → 11.54, houseA 9.15 → 8.07, canopy 15.03 → 14.61, high 10.46 → 9.60), +1.0 to +3.1% frames a second, the same pixels but one (by 1 level of 255, a hit exactly at `cut_out_near`): a hit at distance t hides (1 − t / radius)^1.35 (`AO_TIGHT`: nearness counts more than in proportion, so the shade gathers tight in the contact under a bucket or a tyre and thins quickly away from it; a world that wants deeper contact shade raises Lighting's `AmbientOcclusionIntensity`). It's alpha-blended over what's there, so screen-space ambient occlusion (drawn first when on) shows past the ray tracing distance, and the rays fade into it from 85% of the distance; with screen-space off the target is cleared to nothing hidden first. Ambient occlusion's blur (`OcclusionPasses::blur`) then smooths the result as before.
- **Cost.** At half size, a quarter of a pixel's rays: High (8) costs about as much as two full-size rays a pixel. On the RX 9070 XT, Desert Homestead at 2560 × 1405 with the GPU as the limit (2026-11-25): the rays 2.3 ms, screen-space ambient occlusion under them 0.18 ms, the blur under 0.05 ms.

## Ray traced global illumination (RT2)

Bounced light that follows the world as it changes: light probes lit by rays each frame (the camera's own and any light probe volumes', instead of by their bake), so a moved wall, a lamp turned on or the sun going down colors the light around them. It takes Real-time GI's place (`realtime_gi.rs`, six drawn faces a probe) while on, and is far cheaper per probe.

- **Setting.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_traced_global_illumination` (`RayTracedGlobalIllumination`: Off, Low, Medium, High; `graphics.json`'s `ray_traced_global_illumination`) is Off at every preset. Without ray queries (no RT hardware, or Hardware ray tracing off) it's traced in software (below, RL3) with a note ("Ray traced GI is traced in software: …", `EffectiveGraphics::ray_traced_gi_in_software`), and it's Off only on a GPU without compute shaders either ("Ray traced GI is off: …"). Low, Medium and High trace 128, 256 or 512 probes a frame (`probes_per_frame`) with 64, 128 or 256 rays each (`rays`). It's in Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing, `UserGameSettings.RayTracedGlobalIllumination` (`Enum.RayTracedGlobalIllumination`) and the blocks' "set graphics *RayTracedGlobalIllumination* to". It's left out of the one-click look (its cost depends on the world's volumes); **Ray traced effects off** clears it too.
- **The camera's probes** (`renderer/light_probes.rs`): while it's on, the camera brings grids of its own (cascades), each 16 × 8 × 16 probes (`CAMERA_PROBES`) centred on its spacing nearest the camera: the finest 3 studs apart (a box of 48 × 24 × 48 studs), then 12, 48 and 192 studs apart (`CAMERA_SPACINGS`: 24, 96, 384 and 1,536 studs to either side), as many as reach the ray tracing distance (`cascades_for`: a coarser grid while the one before stops short of it; 4 from 400 studs). Each fades in over two of its spacings from its faces (`CAMERA_BLEND_SPACINGS`), so GI fades into the next coarser grid and, past the last, into the sky, rather than stopping at an edge (before these, GI ended 24 studs from the camera in a hard-looking line in a forest). The finest comes first in the light probes' buffer and is read first, so rooms near the camera get their own light with no volume placed; light probe volumes in the world are drawn and traced next, then the coarser grids, and the sky past them all. With every grid, four of the eight volumes drawn (`MAX_VOLUMES`) are the camera's. They have no bake: each starts untraced. When the camera crosses a spacing, that grid's box moves with it and `follow_camera` copies each probe that stays to its new place on the GPU (each row along X from a copy of the grid, before anything reads it that frame), so only the row it moves into starts again.
- **Probes left out.** The w of a probe's first coefficient is over a half for a probe not to be used: 1 for one a bake found inside a solid (R18.2, which ray traced GI keeps so), 2 for one not traced yet or one ray traced GI finds inside something (with hysteresis: > 35% back-face hits to mark inside, < 20% to recover, `GI_INSIDE_ENTER`/`GI_INSIDE_EXIT`, preventing boundary chatter). `surrounding_diffuse` leaves those out and shares their part among the other probes around the surface; where all eight are left out, the volume gives way to the next or the sky. So probes stuck in walls don't darken the rooms beside them. A probe so marked takes its next update whole. Cut-outs (leaf cards, `SurfaceRaw`'s w) are lit on either side and never count as inside. Bakes are uploaded with 0.
- **The pass** (`renderer/ray_gi.rs`, `ray_gi.wgsl`, compute; `FramePass::RayTracedGi`, "Ray traced GI" in the profiler) runs before the scene. Each probe of the batch (half counting through the near probes, the camera's finest grid and every drawn volume's, and half through the camera's coarser grids, each wrapping round; `LightProbes::near_count`, `Cycle`) traces rays in an upright spherical Fibonacci spiral rotated azimuthally each update (preserving exact 50/50 upper/lower sky-vs-ground elevation balance so flat scenes don't flicker), against the ray traced scene. A hit is lit from its item's surface (`ray_tracing.rs`'s `Surfaces`, one per TLAS slot while this is on: base color times its texture's average color, plus emissive): the sun and up to 16 lamps through shadow rays, plus the probes' own light at the hit (`surrounding_diffuse`), so light bounces again with each update (clamped to `GI_MAX_RADIANCE` to suppress fireflies). Software tracing computes exact geometric world-space triangle normals (`ray_soft.wgsl`) for accurate direct lighting and normal-lifted shadow rays. A miss reads the sky (`sky_specular`, rough). The rays sum into L1 spherical harmonics, blended over what the probe had with adaptive hysteresis (holding strongly at 0.88+ when the sample is within normal Monte Carlo noise of existing probe history, and dropping smoothly toward `keep` when brightness or direction genuinely changes), into the pass's own buffer, then copied over the batch's places in the light probes' buffer (four rgb coefficients a probe, unchanged), so nothing is read back. Turning it off frees it (and the camera's probes) and writes the bakes back.
- **Traced in software** (RL3 of [runtime-lighting-parity.md](../research/runtime-lighting-parity.md), 2026-11-14; `renderer/ray_soft.rs`, `ray_soft.wgsl`): on a GPU without ray queries the same pass runs with a different `gi_trace` (`ray_gi_software.wgsl` in place of `ray_gi_hardware.wgsl`; `gpu::RAY_GI_SOFTWARE_SHADER`), so the probes, cascades, flags and shading are RT2's. Its rays walk BVHs built on the CPU and kept in one storage buffer of `vec4`s (bound where the TLAS is; layout in `ray_soft.wgsl`): one per mesh from its simplest level of detail (the last `MeshSource::lod`; GI's light is soft), split at the median along its widest axis, four triangles a leaf, built the first frame it's wanted up to `TRIANGLES_PER_FRAME` (100,000 a frame; a bigger mesh alone on a frame of its own) and again when its version changes; then the items within the ray tracing distance (their world-to-mesh rows, mesh, mask, slot) under a top BVH made again each frame and written only when it changed. The meshes' part is rewritten only when a mesh comes or goes. Items' colors are `Surfaces` of the software scene's own. The walk is a stack of 32 (`MOST_DEPTH` 28 keeps every BVH within it), front faces are the counter-clockwise side as in the hardware's scene (so probes inside blocks are found the same way), and shadow rays stop at their first hit. Left out against the hardware: posed characters (their rest pose would be in the wrong place), swaying plants' bending (traced still) and cut-outs' holes, which are guessed: a ray passes each cut-out triangle it meets half the time, by a hash of the ray, so a canopy lets about half the light through. The scene is freed with the setting, or once the GPU has ray queries. Settings > Graphics' In use line says "GI … traced in software".
- **Cost.** A probe costs its rays, a few hundred, instead of six drawn faces: High (512 × 256 rays) is about 131,000 rays a frame (plus a shadow ray toward the sun and each lamp from every hit), a small fraction of a 1440p shadow pass. Medium goes round the camera's finest 2,048 probes every 16 frames, and round its coarser ones (6,144 at 400 studs) every 48. Numbers from the PC check.

## Ray traced reflections (RL4)

Glossy surfaces mirror what's around them with a ray from each pixel (RL4 of [runtime-lighting-parity.md](../research/runtime-lighting-parity.md), 2026-11-14): what's off the screen, or out of every reflection probe's box, shows too, and a part's reflection moves with it.

- **Setting.** `GraphicsSettings::ray_traced_reflections` (`RayTracedReflections`: Off, Low, Medium, High; `graphics.json`'s `ray_traced_reflections`) is Off at every preset and Off without ray queries, with a note ("Ray traced reflections are off: … Reflection probes are used."). It picks which surfaces trace (`roughness_cutoff`: smoother than 0.15, 0.3 or 0.5) and how many pixels (`downscale`: Low traces at half size, one ray for 2 × 2 pixels; Medium and High at full size). It's in Settings > Graphics > Ray tracing, `UserGameSettings.RayTracedReflections` (`Enum.RayTracedReflections`) and the blocks' "set graphics *RayTracedReflections* to"; the editor's In use line and the viewport badge ("Reflections Medium") list it. It's left out of the one-click look; **Ray traced effects off** clears it.
- **Roughness in the prepass.** `fs_prepass` writes a plain solid pixel's normal w as 1 plus its roughness (0 to 0.999; it was 1), terrain's as 1.999, so it isn't traced. Characters (0.5) and cut-outs (0.875) keep their marks and aren't traced; every other reader of w compares to those or to "1 or more", so nothing else changes. The prepass is drawn while it's on (`targets_used`).
- **The pass** (`renderer/ray_reflections.rs`, `ray_reflections.wgsl`, `FramePass::RayTracedReflections`, "Ray traced reflections" in the profiler, after ray traced GI) is a compute pass over the traced size (8 × 8 groups). Each traced pixel rebuilds its world point from the prepass depth (`clip_to_world`, the frame's jittered matrix inverted), skips the sky, rough, character, cut-out and terrain pixels and those past the ray tracing distance, and traces one ray along the mirrored view through solid items and cut-outs (mask 27; through cut-outs' holes, `trace_through_holes`; see-through items and water aren't seen, as they're drawn after the scene that reads this). A hit is lit as ray traced GI lights one (`reflect_hit_light`, as water's `water_hit_light`: its color with its texture's average and glow from the ray traced scene's `Surfaces`, the sun with a shadow ray, the first 8 lamps in reach with theirs, the flat ambient and the light probes), fogged on the way back. One direction per pixel, the mirror's, not spread by roughness: rough surfaces fade into the probes instead (below), so there's no noise to smooth. A miss, a hit from inside something solid, or a pixel not traced writes a weight of 0.
- **What the scene reads.** Results are two `u32`s a traced pixel (rgb and a weight, as half floats) in a storage buffer at frame binding 18 (the item shader's 16 textures are all used; a one-entry stand-in while off). `lighting.wgsl`'s `traced_reflected`, in `shade_surface`'s reflection, mixes the traced light over the reflection probes and sky (`reflected`) by its weight times (1 − roughness / cutoff)², so the look changes smoothly up to the cutoff, and only for solid surfaces (see-through ones and water keep the probes). The weight fades out from 85% of the ray tracing distance. `Frame::ray_reflections` is (cutoff, downscale, traced width, unused); a cutoff of 0 (off, or nothing traced this frame) reads nothing.
- **Screen-space reflections** ([reflections.md](reflections.md), 2026-11-26) use the same buffer and `traced_reflected` while ray traced reflections aren't drawn; when these are, they take its place.
- **Solid pixels everywhere (fix, 2026-11-14).** `VertexOut::base_color` is now flat-interpolated: it's the item's own, and interpolated across a big triangle its opacity came out a hair under 1 in scattered runs of pixels, which then weren't `solid`, so they missed screen-space and ray traced ambient occlusion, ray traced shadows and now these reflections. The mirror test found it.
- **Cost.** One ray a pixel (a quarter at Low), plus a shadow ray toward the sun and each lamp from every hit, for glossy pixels only; rough and matte ones return before tracing. Numbers from the PC check.
