# Graphics settings

The machine's graphics choices: presets, overrides, what the GPU can do, and who changes them. How each setting draws is in the file for its feature (listed at the end).

## Look and cost

Two kinds of setting, never mixed:

- **Look** belongs to the world: Lighting (`ToneMapping`, `Exposure`, bloom, `EnvironmentIntensity`, `SkyImage`, `AmbientOcclusionIntensity`, shadows) and the lights. It's saved in `world.json`, and Lua, blocks and the Properties panel change it.
- **Cost** belongs to the machine: `render::GraphicsSettings` (from `render-types`) (`render-types/src/graphics.rs`). It's saved per machine (the editor's settings, the player's `graphics.json`), never in a project.

Where a Lighting value is also a cost, the world's value is a request and the settings are a ceiling: shadows draw only if `ShadowsEnabled` and the shadow quality allow them, out to the smaller of the two distances (`lighting::shadows_drawn`), and post-processing runs only if `PostProcessingEnabled` and the settings allow it (`lighting::post_drawn`). No Lighting property changes meaning.

## Presets and overrides

`GraphicsSettings` is a `quality` preset plus an optional override for each value. An override replaces only its own value, and changing the preset keeps the overrides (in the editor and from Lua alike).

| Post-processing | off | on | on | on |
| Render scale (50% to 200%) | 75% | 100% | 100% | 100% |

| Mesh memory budget (half the texture memory budget; no override of its own) | 128 MB | 256 MB | 512 MB | 1024 MB |

**The memory budgets follow the card's memory** (ADR-0051). With no texture memory budget set, the budget is a quarter of the card's memory, counted in whole GB as its box says and rounded down to a power of two between 256 MB and 8 GB (`texture_budget_for`), wherever that is more than the preset's number above: 4096 MB on a 16 GB card at every preset, 2048 MB on an 8 GB one, and the preset's own on a card of 4 GB or less, so no card gets less than it did (`GraphicsSettings::texture_budget_on`). A budget that is set is used as set. The card's memory is `Capabilities::video_memory`, read once per card by `render::video_memory` from the display driver's registry entry on Windows (two `reg` queries, the first time a renderer is made) and amdgpu's `mem_info_vram_total` on Linux; other cards, and software drawing, don't say, and get the preset's. A `TextureRenderer` is made from a device alone, so it asks for the card a device was last opened on (`adapters::opened_card_memory`; every device is opened through `adapters::with_ray_queries`). The player logs what the budgets came to (`RUST_LOG=info`).

**When a world needs more** (`RenderStats::memory_warnings`): `RenderStats::texture_bytes_wanted` is what the textures held would take with no level left out, and `freed_for_budget` counts the meshes and textures freed to fit a budget since the settings last changed. The editor's Graphics page shows a warning under the quality when the textures wanted are over the texture budget (they are drawn at lower detail), when meshes are over theirs, or when anything was freed to fit, each saying what to change.

`GraphicsSettings::ray_traced()` is the editor's **Use the ray traced look**: Hardware ray tracing, ray traced shadows and AO at Medium, shadow and reflection quality at least High and TAA, keeping every other field; `without_ray_traced_effects()` clears the ray traced effects' overrides (shadows, AO, GI) ([ray-tracing.md](ray-tracing.md)).

High is the look worlds were made with. `automatic` (Lua's `Enum.GraphicsQuality.Automatic`) means the quality follows the machine: `GraphicsSettings::automatic(&Capabilities)` is High, or Low on a software adapter (`DeviceType::Cpu`), with no overrides. It's what a machine starts with (`default_for`), saved as `"automatic": true`, and picked again for the machine on each start (`player::graphics::fit_machine`).

## Capabilities

`render::Capabilities` is read from the device once (`Capabilities::read`): the largest 2D texture and array, timestamp queries, whether it's a software adapter, and the sample counts both scene formats support. `GraphicsSettings::effective(&Capabilities)` gives the `EffectiveGraphics` the renderer uses: a shadow quality whose maps don't fit drops to the best that does (a point light's six faces are six layers), an MSAA count the GPU lacks drops to the next mode down (4× → 2× → FXAA), a reflection cubemap larger than the GPU's textures drops to one that fits (or Low), whether the device can trace rays (`Capabilities::ray_queries`, [ray-tracing.md](ray-tracing.md)), and real-time GI is off on a GPU without compute shaders (`Capabilities::compute_shaders`: no compute workgroups or storage buffers in its limits, as on WebGL 2), each with a note saying why. It never raises a value. The render scale is held to the largest texture the GPU can make (`scene::scene_size`), and textures leave out levels wider than it (`gpu::create_texture`).

`Renderer::set_graphics` and `TextureRenderer::set_graphics` apply settings from the next frame; settings equal to the last ones do nothing. Every frame target exists only while a setting uses it, and a change frees what's no longer used. `TextureRenderer::limit_capabilities` draws as if the GPU could do less (each value the lower of the two), for checking fallbacks on a GPU that can do more.

## Who changes them

- **Editor**: Settings > Graphics (`editor/src/app/settings/graphics.rs`) is grouped by what the settings draw: **Quality** (the preset, and a world in the viewport that needs more than the memory budgets said so under it), **Resolution** (Render scale, Anti-aliasing), **Lighting** (Shadow quality, Ambient occlusion, and one **Global illumination** menu: baked probes or ray traced Low, Medium or High, writing `ray_traced_global_illumination`; traced in software on a GPU with compute shaders but no ray queries), **Reflections** (**Sky and probe reflections** is `reflection_quality`; Screen-space reflections says "Replaced by ray traced reflections" while those are on, as the renderer then skips it, `screen_reflections::marched`), **Atmosphere** (Clouds, Volumetric fog), **Ray tracing** (one status tag, Hardware ray tracing with Restart now, ray traced shadows, ambient occlusion and reflections, Turn all off; "Needs hardware ray tracing" said once), **Camera effects** (**Bloom and tone mapping** is `post_processing`, which gates only those; Depth of field; Motion blur; one **Lens effects** menu setting vignette, film grain, chromatic aberration, sharpen and sun rays together, "Mixed" while they differ), **Advanced**, folded shut unless search shows one of its rows (Shadow distance, Far shadow detail, Contact shadows, Texture quality, Texture memory, Ray tracing distance, Ray traced shadow resolution, Real-time GI (raster), and the five lens switches apart), and **In use** (tags for the preset, scale, anti-aliasing, shadows, the memory budgets and ray tracing, and `EffectiveGraphics::notes`). The groups are UI only: every `GraphicsSettings` field keeps its name in `graphics.json` and Lua. Every control has a **cost tag** (`Cost`: Free, Low under about 0.5 ms, Medium 0.5 to 2 ms, High over 2 ms or heavy CPU work), whose hover gives the measurement on the RX 9070 XT it comes from ([performance.md](performance.md), the wiki's Benchmark History): Render scale High (Desert Homestead's GPU frame 3.75 ms plus 1.09 ms a million pixels), Shadow quality High (no shadows 4.4 to 5.0 ms less, High instead of Ultra 2.6 to 2.9 ms less at 1440p), Shadow distance Low (shorter isn't cheaper), Contact shadows Low, Far shadow detail Low (on saves the farthest cascade 0.5–0.9 ms in Overgrown Street, 1% lows up 6–13%), Anti-aliasing Medium (MSAA 4× 0.7 to 1.5 ms at 1440p), Ambient occlusion Low (0.1 to 0.2 ms), Sky and probe reflections Free (video memory), Screen-space reflections Medium (about 1 ms at 1440p, Overgrown Street), Clouds Low (0.14 to 0.30 ms at 1080p Ultra), Volumetric fog Low (0.38 ms), Real-time GI (raster) High (CPU; once 434 ms a frame), Bloom and tone mapping Low (0.1 ms), Depth of field and Motion blur Low, the lens effects and texture settings Free, Hardware ray tracing Free by itself, Ray tracing distance High (CPU; Overgrown Street's ray scene 8.6 ms of the frame's thread), Ray traced shadows High (4.7 ms in Desert Homestead; 12.8 ms Full, 5.5 Half in Overgrown Street), Ray traced shadow resolution Medium (Half about 55% less), Ray traced ambient occlusion High (2.3 to 2.9 ms; 6.0 in Overgrown Street), ray traced reflections and GI Low (0 to 2 fps in Desert Homestead, not measured elsewhere). The tags inform; no preset value follows them. The Texture memory row reads "Automatic (4096 MB)" while the budget comes from the card's memory and "Preset (1024 MB)" while it is the preset's (hover says why). Each preset-or-override menu is one helper (`choice`), each number with a preset (`custom_value`) another, and the page fits a 260-point column (`responsive::form`). It's saved with the editor's settings, and the viewport uses it at once.
- **Editor benchmark**: Debug > **Graphics Benchmark…** (`editor-benchmark/src/lib.rs`, any build) measures how this computer draws the viewport and recommends the *editor's* settings only. It makes its own `TextureRenderer` on the editor's device and draws worlds of its own built in memory (`editor-benchmark/src/workloads.rs`; the open project, its Lighting and the game's `graphics.json` are never read or written), at the viewport's size: a test town (about 2,000 parts of every shape, glass, neon and metal, a sun, 16 lamps with 6 casting shadows, an Atmosphere and Clouds) at each preset, Low to Ultra, shown as Performance, Balanced, High Quality and Maximum Quality, then Geometry (20,000 parts), Materials (every material in 8 variants) and Lights (80 lamps, 24 with shadows) at the recommended settings. The camera circles each world (`ORBIT_FRAMES`), the same views for every preset. Per frame it times the draw list and recording on the CPU and the GPU's own timestamps per pass (`RenderStats`), waiting for the GPU after each frame; a frame is the longer of the two (the whole wait without timestamps). Each measurement keeps the median, 95th and 99th percentile and worst frame, frames over twice the median (hitches), the first frame after the switch (pipelines, shaders, targets), the GPU memory the renderer holds and the passes' medians; making the renderer is timed as setup. A preset over three times the budget skips the ones above it. The rules (`editor-benchmark/src/recommend.rs`): the best preset whose 95th-percentile frame is within 11.1 ms (two-thirds of a 60 fps frame); when the next preset up misses, it is measured again with the setting whose pass grew most set back (shadows, ambient occlusion, volumetric fog, anti-aliasing or clouds) and recommended if that fits; when even Low misses, a GPU-bound machine gets a lower render scale (by the square root of the time over, 50% at least) and a CPU-bound one is told settings won't help much; a software adapter gets Low. The texture budget is set to a quarter of the card's memory, counted in whole GB as its box says (a power of two, 256 MB to 8 GB), only where that is lower than the preset's: where it is higher, the settings take it by themselves with no budget set. The card's memory comes from the display driver's registry entry on Windows and amdgpu's `mem_info_vram_total` on Linux (`render/src/video_memory.rs`, no unsafe code), otherwise it's not reported. The window lists the reasons, a Now/Recommended table of every setting that would change, **Apply Recommended** (Undo puts the old settings back; it keeps what the benchmark doesn't weigh as the person set it: real-time GI, the lens effects and motion blur, and a texture budget of at most half the card's memory, `recommend::apply_to`), **Keep Current Settings** and **Run Again**; nothing changes without Apply, and starting is refused during Play. The last report is saved with the editor's settings (`graphics benchmark` key, versioned) and shown when the window opens again, with a warning if it was measured on another graphics card.
- **Player**: `graphics.json` in the game's player data folder (`save::game_folder`, `player/src/graphics.rs`). The first start writes Automatic. A file that doesn't read as settings is warned about and replaced by the default. The notes go to the log.
- **LocalScripts**: `game:GetService("UserGameSettings")` (`script/src/user_settings.rs`) has `GraphicsQuality` (`Enum.GraphicsQuality`: Automatic, Low, Medium, High, Ultra) and the overrides `ShadowQuality` (`Enum.ShadowQuality`), `ShadowDistance`, `PostProcessing`, `RenderScale` (kept within 0.5 to 2), `TextureQuality` (`Enum.TextureQuality`), `AntiAliasing` (`Enum.AntiAliasing`: Off, FXAA, MSAA2, MSAA4, TAA), `AmbientOcclusion` (`Enum.AmbientOcclusion`), `ReflectionQuality` (`Enum.ReflectionQuality`: Low, Medium, High, Ultra), `CloudQuality` (`Enum.CloudQuality`: Low, Medium, High, Ultra), `DepthOfField` (`Enum.DepthOfField`: Off, Low, High), `MotionBlur` (`Enum.MotionBlur`: Off, Low, High), `RayTracedShadows` (`Enum.RayTracedShadows`: Off, Low, Medium, High; Off on a GPU that can't ray trace), `RayTracedAmbientOcclusion` (`Enum.RayTracedAmbientOcclusion`, the same choices), `RayTracedGlobalIllumination` (`Enum.RayTracedGlobalIllumination`, the same choices), `RayTracedReflections` (`Enum.RayTracedReflections`, the same choices; Off on a GPU that can't ray trace), `HardwareRayTracing` (true or false: whether the GPU is opened with ray queries, saved at once and applied at the game's next start, RL2), the read-only `RayTracingSupported` (`Capabilities::ray_tracing_offered`: the GPU could ray trace) and `RayTracingActive` (`Capabilities::ray_queries`: it was opened with them this run; a settings menu tells the player to restart while it differs from `HardwareRayTracing`), and `ContactShadows`, `RealtimeGI`, `VolumetricFog`, `Vignette`, `FilmGrain`, `ChromaticAberration`, `Sharpen` and `SunRays` (true or false). Reading one gives the value chosen (the override, or the preset's), not what the GPU lowered it to, and setting an override to nil goes back to the preset's. The texture memory budget and the ray tracing distance aren't among them: they're the machine's to set, in the editor or `graphics.json` (`texture_budget`, in MB; `ray_tracing_distance`, in studs). Nor, yet, is screen-space reflections (`screen_space_reflections` in `graphics.json`, 2026-11-26). A Script gets "UserGameSettings only works in a LocalScript…". Hosts hand the settings in with `ScriptRuntime::set_graphics(settings, capabilities)` before scripts start (the capabilities pick Automatic's quality) and take a change with `take_graphics_change`. The player then fits it to the machine, saves it to `graphics.json` and draws with it from that frame. The editor's Play starts from the editor's own settings, draws a script's change in the viewport (`Session::graphics`) and drops it at Stop. Blocks: "set graphics quality to", "set graphics *option* to" (a choice, or Preset for nil) and "set graphics *number* to" ([scripting/blocks.md](../scripting/blocks.md)). `crates/samples/projects/graphics`' LocalScript steps through the presets on G.

## Tests

- `render-types/src/graphics.rs`: each preset's values, overrides, the `graphics.json` shape, and `effective` lowering and never raising.
- `player/src/graphics_tests.rs`: the `graphics` sample matches `reference-low.png`, `reference-medium.png`, `reference.png` (High, also checked by `every_example_renders`) and `reference-ultra.png`, at 480×270 within 6 per channel on average. After a deliberate change to the look, run `hello_graphics_matches_each_presets_reference` with `AXCENITY_WRITE_REFERENCES` set to rewrite Low, Medium and Ultra (High is rewritten as [pipeline.md](pipeline.md) says), and check them by eye. Also: lighting changes move every kind of material the same way, a LocalScript's changes show in the next frame and are saved, and a GPU limited to no MSAA and 1024² (then 64²) textures draws with fallbacks and notes.
- `editor-benchmark/src/recommend.rs` and `tests.rs`, `editor/src/app/graphics_benchmark_tests.rs`: each recommendation rule on made-up numbers, a whole quick run on the real device (every preset in order, the heavier tests at the recommendation, the report saved and read back), the changes table having a row for every setting, Apply keeping real-time GI, the lens effects and a budget that fits, the window in each state, a run leaving the project and the settings alone until Apply (and Undo), the report surviving a restart, and no start during Play.
- `editor/src/app/settings/tests.rs`: `every_graphics_row_has_a_cost_tag_and_fits_a_narrow_column` (every row drawn has its tag, every searchable Graphics row is one, Advanced opens for search, 260 points wide), the memory warnings and Texture memory's text; `app/search_tests.rs`' `every_searchable_setting_is_found_on_its_page` finds each row, the Advanced ones too.
- `render/src/renderer/tests.rs`: `switching_every_setting_and_dropping_renderers_leaves_no_resources_behind` switches every setting through every value, checks each target and map is alive only while used and that the caches come back to their starting counts, then drops the renderer and checks, with wgpu's report (`Instance::generate_report`), that the device holds no more textures, views, buffers, bind groups, pipelines, samplers or query sets than before.
