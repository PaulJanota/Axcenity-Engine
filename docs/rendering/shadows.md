# Shadows

Sun shadows and contact shadows. Spot and point light shadows are in [lights.md](lights.md); the shadow quality setting sizes all three maps.

- `DrawList::shadows` (`Shadows { enabled, distance, softness }`) comes from the `Lighting` service's `ShadowsEnabled`, `ShadowDistance` and `ShadowSoftness` properties (`scene` reads them). The defaults are on, 150 studs, softness 0.2.
- **Graphics settings** (the presets, overrides, capabilities, `UserGameSettings` and where they're saved: [settings.md](settings.md)) are the machine's ceiling on the world's request: shadows draw only if both `ShadowsEnabled` and the settings' shadow quality allow them, out to the smaller of `ShadowDistance` and the settings' shadow distance (`lighting::shadows_drawn`). Shadow quality picks the map: Off (no map is allocated), Low 1024² × 2 cascades, Medium 2048² × 2, High 2048² × 3 (today's look), Ultra 4096² × 4. `SHADOW_MAP_SIZE` and `SHADOW_CASCADES` are the maximums (the shader's `cascades` array holds 4). `effective` lowers a quality whose maps the device can't hold (`Capabilities::max_texture_size`, `max_texture_layers`) to the best that fits, with a note. A map of the wrong size is freed and remade at the new size when next drawn, and a distance ceiling of 0 draws no shadows and makes no map.
- **Render scale and texture quality** (graphics Phase 3) are graphics settings too. Render scale (50% to 200%; Low 75%, the other presets 100%) sizes the scene's HDR, depth and bloom targets (`FrameTargets`, at `scene::scene_size`: the output's size times the scale, at most the GPU's largest texture). Lights are tiled in the scene's pixels; the composite pass's linear sampler scales the scene to the output, and the UI is always drawn at the output's size. Bloom blurs down to `BLOOM_LEVELS` halvings of the output's size whatever the scale, so the glow spreads as far. A new scale or output size remakes the targets and frees the old. Picking is unaffected: it works from the viewport's rectangle (`editor::screen`). Texture quality (Full, Half, Quarter; Ultra and High Full, Medium Half, Low Quarter) leaves out that many of a texture's largest mip levels at upload (`gpu::create_texture`), and halves terrain layer arrays the same way. UI images and terrain weight maps keep their full size (`ScenePipeline::full_textures`). A new quality empties the scene's texture and material caches (and the sprite and terrain bindings using them), so textures are uploaded again at the new size. `RenderStats::texture_bytes` and `mesh_bytes` count what's on the GPU, added at upload and taken off when freed (`GpuBytes`); the editor's Profiler shows them.
- **Texture memory budget** (graphics Phase 15; Low 256 MB, Medium 512, High 1024, Ultra 2048, MB being MiB, or a quarter of the card's memory where that is more, [settings.md](settings.md)) caps `texture_bytes`. After each frame's textures are noted and before its materials are bound, a renderer that keeps what's undrawn first frees color textures not drawn for 10 seconds, the least recently drawn first, until textures fit (`free_undrawn_textures`, ADR-0051, [models.md](models.md)); then `ScenePipeline::fit_texture_budget` (`renderer/texture_budget.rs`) works out how many of each surface texture's largest mip levels to leave out (`levels_to_drop`): none while everything fits; otherwise the least recently drawn lose a level at a time first (a texture kept but not drawn lately goes before one drawn this frame) and, among those drawn as recently, the largest. Each keeps its 1×1 level. Shrinking copies the smaller levels into a new texture on the GPU (`gpu::shrink_texture`), so the caller's pixels aren't needed. A shrunk texture drawn this frame whose full size fits again is uploaded again at that size. The materials and sprite bindings of a replaced texture are remade. UI images, terrain and the white texture count toward the budget but are never shrunk, so if they alone pass it, every surface texture ends at its smallest. Within the budget the check is one pass over the cache, and nothing changes.
- **Anti-aliasing** (graphics Phase 4; `AntiAliasing`: Off, FXAA, MSAA 2×, MSAA 4×, and from graphics Phase 35 TAA, which no preset picks ([temporal.md](temporal.md)); Ultra and High MSAA 4×, Medium FXAA, Low Off) is a graphics setting too. MSAA draws the scene pass into a multisampled `Rgba16Float` color and depth (`FrameTargets::msaa`, `depth_view`), resolved into the single-sample HDR texture that bloom reads; the sky, solid, see-through, terrain and sprite pipelines are built for the current sample count (`scene::scene_pipelines`, `TerrainPass::set_samples`, `SpritePass::set_samples`) and rebuilt when it changes. Shadow passes stay single-sample. MSAA smooths triangles' edges, not the holes a texture cuts in them, so a standard material's cut-out (`alpha_cutoff`) depth fill turns its edge into coverage when multisampled (`fs_depth_coverage`, `alpha_to_coverage_enabled` on `DepthUse::Fill`): the alpha it returns ramps from 0 to 1 over one pixel around the cut-off, the GPU writes depth to that share of the samples, and `fs_prefilled` lights the samples whose depth is its own. With one sample per pixel the fill discards at the cut-off instead (`fs_depth_only`). A hair card's coverage is its opacity itself, and under TAA its alpha is hashed ([hair.md](hair.md)). A surface shader's cut-outs still discard per pixel, so theirs aren't smoothed. `Capabilities::msaa_samples` holds the sample counts both scene formats support (`graphics::SCENE_FORMATS`); devices are opened without adapter-specific format features, so these are wgpu's guaranteed ones (1 and 4). `effective` lowers a count the GPU lacks to the next mode down (4× → 2× → FXAA) with a note. FXAA runs after the composite pass: the composite draws into an `Rgba8UnormSrgb` target at the output's size (`FrameTargets::fxaa`, `FXAA_FORMAT`), then `fs_fxaa` in `post.wgsl` smooths it into the output before the UI. The multisampled and FXAA targets exist only while their mode is on; a new mode remakes the targets and frees the old (`TargetsAlive` counts them for tests).
- **Ambient occlusion** (graphics Phase 9; `AmbientOcclusion`: Off, Low, High; Ultra and High High, Medium Low, Low Off) is a graphics setting too; every GPU can draw it, so `effective` never lowers it. While it's on, `FrameTargets::occlusion` (`OcclusionTargets`, `renderer/occlusion.rs`) holds a depth and normal prepass at the scene's size (single-sampled `Depth32Float`, kept for reading, and view-space normals in `Rgba16Float`) and two `R8Unorm` occlusion targets at half that size; they're made and freed with the other frame targets (`TargetsAlive::occlusion` counts them). Each frame, after the shadow passes, the prepass draws solid batches (items with `fs_prepass` in shader.wgsl, cutting out what `alpha_cutoff` cuts, double-sided ones with both faces; terrain with `fs_terrain_prepass`), so `RenderStats::draw_calls` counts those draws too. `ssao.wgsl`'s `fs_occlusion` then tests a hemisphere of 8 (Low) or 16 (High) points around each pixel's normal, out to `OCCLUSION_RADIUS` (1.5 studs), turned per pixel by the same interleaved gradient noise as shadows, with a range check so a part far in front doesn't shade what's behind it, and `fs_blur` averages 3×3 of the result, leaving out pixels at a different distance. The scene pass reads it (frame bindings 7 and 8, a 1×1 white stand-in while it's off) through `screen_occlusion` in lighting.wgsl, raised to the world's strength, and multiplies it into the `occlusion` that `light_surface` applies to ambient and sky light only, so sun, lamp and emissive light are unchanged. Leaves (translucent materials) take its square root (`LEAF_SCREEN_OCCLUSION`, [models.md](models.md)), since a crown of cut-out cards isn't the solid heap the prepass sees; what they cast on others is unchanged. Small things on the ground are caught by it down to about a tenth of a stud from their foot (a quarter-stud post darkens the floor 0.14 studs off to under 0.8 of its light; `renderer/tests/foliage_light.rs`); a tuft of grass that flares out from a narrow foot shades the ground round it only a little, and is grounded by its own foot shade instead (`LEAF_FOOT_SHADE_LOW`). Contact shadows only shade sun and lamp light, so under an overcast sky they do little. See-through items (`base_color.a < 1`) and the sky don't read it. The world's look is the Lighting property `AmbientOcclusionIntensity` (0 to 2, default 1; `AmbientLight::occlusion` in the draw list, clamped by `scene` and `lighting::occlusion_drawn`): 0 is no darkening, 2 squares it. The prepass's depth is there for later screen-space effects.
- **Contact shadows** (graphics Phase 18; `contact_shadows`, on at High and Ultra, off at Low and Medium) catch the small gaps the shadow maps are too coarse for, such as under a part resting on the floor. They're drawn when the world's `ShadowsEnabled` is on and the setting allows them (`lighting::contact_drawn`), whatever the shadow maps' quality. They need the prepass's depth, so the prepass and its targets (`FrameTargets::occlusion`) exist while ambient occlusion or contact shadows are on; with only contact shadows, the ambient occlusion passes aren't drawn and the scene reads the white stand-in for occlusion. The prepass draws with its own frame bindings (`prepass_frame_bind_group`) holding a 1×1 stand-in at binding 15, as it can't read the depth it's drawing; the scene pass reads the prepass depth there (`prepass_depth` in lighting.wgsl). For each solid surface (see-through ones aren't in the prepass), `contact_visibility` takes 12 steps toward the sun, and toward each lamp that casts shadows, over `CONTACT_SHADOW_LENGTH` (1 stud, or the distance to the lamp if less), starting a little off the surface and jittered per pixel by the interleaved noise. A step blocked by what's on screen there (behind it by more than a bias that grows with distance, and by less than `CONTACT_SHADOW_THICKNESS`, 0.5 studs, so a part well in front doesn't count) shades the surface, darkest where the blocker is nearest and fading out toward the end of the march. They multiply only that light's direct share, like the shadow maps. `Frame::contact` holds on or off, the length and the thickness.
- **Cascades**: at High, three 2048² depth maps (layers of one array texture) each cover a slice of the view out to `distance`, nearest first. The slices' ends are mostly geometric (`CASCADE_SPLIT_BLEND`), so the first covers only the closest dozen or so studs and is the sharpest. Each map covers a sphere around its slice (so turning the camera doesn't change its scale), snapped to whole texels so edges don't shimmer. Casters up to 500 studs toward the sun are included. Each cascade draws only the casters whose bounding spheres reach its box, whether or not the camera sees them, so a part behind the camera still shadows what's in view (see Frustum culling in [pipeline.md](pipeline.md)). The shader uses the nearest cascade that covers a point and blends into the next over the outer 20% of each, so the change in sharpness doesn't show.
- **Soft shadows** (percentage-closer soft shadows): the shader first searches the map around the point for what blocks the sun and averages its depth, then blurs over a width proportional to the distance from that blocker to the surface times the sun's apparent size (`ShadowSoftness` × `MAX_SUN_SIZE`). So shadows are crisp where a part touches what it shades and soften further away, like real ones; softness 0 is crisp everywhere. Both steps take 16 samples on a Poisson disc, turned per pixel by interleaved gradient noise, and each comparison is itself filtered between four texels. The blur is at most 32 texels.
- **Per-pixel noise under TAA** (2026-11-17, character refinement CO7): the shadow filters' Poisson turn, contact shadows' step jitter and the lamps' shadow turn read `shading_noise` (lighting.wgsl), interleaved gradient noise moved on by Jimenez's step (5.588238 pixels) each frame while TAA is on (`Frame::noise.x`, the TAA frame index mod 64; 0 otherwise, so other modes keep the fixed pattern and don't flicker). Before, the pattern stood still under TAA's jitter, so TAA kept it: a nearby arm's contact shadow on the flank showed as diagonal hatching and soft edges as stipple. Now TAA averages it to a smooth edge (the arm's false contact shadow is softened, not removed). Without TAA (Off, FXAA, MSAA; High and Ultra default to MSAA 4×) the fixed pattern is unchanged, so a still frame draws the same pixels every time and the hatching still shows there. `ScenePipeline::shading_noise_step` picks the value; tests: `with_taa_the_shadow_noise_moves_every_frame` and `without_taa_soft_and_contact_shadow_edges_draw_the_same_pixels_every_frame` (`renderer/tests/point_and_contact_shadows.rs`). Evidence: `docs/development/character-studio/refinement/co7/`.
- **Bumps at the terminator** (2026-11-17): a normal-mapped surface keeps `bump_shadowing` of each sun and lamp light (Conty Estevez, Lecocq and Stein's microfacet shadowing for bump maps, Ray Tracing Gems 2019): the mapped normal's lean from the surface's own becomes a roughness, and light grazing the surface is shadowed by the bumps; where the surface itself faces away from the light nothing is lit. Without it each bump's own n·l decided alone and the line where light ends broke up into lit and unlit speckles (on the realistic skin's grain, 31% less pixel-to-pixel noise across the terminator in the hard-light face capture). Only a normal map's normal gets it: `light_surface` and `shade_surface` take `bumped`, which the standard item shader sets when its material has a normal map (`Maps::settings.x`, the map's scale, isn't 0). Every other leaned shading normal is lit as before: leaves' crown-bent normal (`lit_leaf`), water's waves, decals, terrain (its layers' normal maps too, left as they were) and a surface shader's own normal without a normal map. An earlier version applied it to all of them and took up to 61% of the sun from the sunlit edge of tree crowns. Tests in `renderer/tests/shading.rs`: `a_normal_mapped_surface_keeps_a_clean_terminator_under_a_grazing_sun` (nothing lit past the surface's own terminator, 25 against 38 pixel-to-pixel noise across it), `a_leafs_sunlit_crown_edge_is_lit_whichever_side_of_its_card_the_sun_is` and `a_surface_without_a_normal_map_is_lit_by_its_own_shading_normal`.
- **Micro-shadowing** (2026-11-27, `micro_shadow` in lighting.wgsl): in a surface's cracks and pits, where its own maps say it's closed in, direct light coming in at a slant is shaded by the relief around it (Chan 2018, "Material Advances in Call of Duty: WWII", as Filament's `computeMicroShadowing`): the cone of directions a pit is open to narrows with its occlusion `o` (`1 / sqrt(1 - o)`), and the sun's and each lamp's light is multiplied by `saturate(n·l / sqrt(1 - o))²`, after the bump shadowing. What's open is `micro_open`, a private value the shaders set before lighting a pixel, 1 (nothing changes) unless set: the standard item shader sets it to the material's ambient occlusion map (with its strength) when it has one. Water and snow fill it as they fill occlusion (`filled_occlusion`). Never screen-space occlusion, which isn't the surface's own relief. Parts and meshes have no height map of their own (and the item shader has no texture binding left for one), so their occlusion map stands for it. The terrain doesn't set it: its layers' relief already shades it (the reference this matches), and giving it the layers' height map as well darkened whole layers whose heights sit low (Desert Homestead's dirt, about 6 levels across a view from above), not only their cracks. Leaves, hair, skin, cloth, water and decals don't set it. Test: `a_surfaces_occlusion_map_shades_slanting_sunlight_in_its_cracks` (`renderer/tests/shading.rs`: open or with no map, and with the sun straight on, nothing changes; in a crack at 78° the sun gives 17 against 72).
- **Shadows a ScatterRule's plants cast** (2026-11-27): a ScatterRule's `CastShadow` (on by default) and a Scatter palette item's **Shadow** are `FoliageInstance::shadow`, which each draw item made from the plant or model takes as `DrawItem::casts_shadow`: off, it's in no shadow map and ray traced shadows pass it, as a Part with `CastShadow` off. For ground cover too small for its shadow to show (fine grass, weeds, leaf litter), which contact shadows still ground where the sun isn't ray traced. Overgrown Street turns it off for five rules ([performance.md](performance.md)). Tests: `foliage_that_casts_no_shadow_draws_items_that_cast_none` (`scene/src/foliage.rs`), `generated_foliage_is_never_saved_and_goes_with_its_rules` (`procedural`), the terrain and palette files' round trips.
- **Acne and peter-panning**: the point is offset along its surface normal by 1.5 texels, the depth bias grows with the blur width and the surface's slope to the sun, and the shadow pass adds a slope-scaled hardware bias.
- **Far shadow detail** (`shadow_lod`, on at every preset, 2026-11-28): a caster is drawn at the level of detail the camera gives it, so the farthest cascade, which takes in every caster near the camera too, drew the trees beside the camera at full detail (in Overgrown Street 43% of its 21.5 million triangles were full detail, though those trees are a few of its texels across and their shadows are read from the nearer cascades). With the setting on, the farthest cascade draws each caster at the level its size in that map calls for (`lighting::shadow_level`: its bounding sphere's width as a share of the map's width, against the camera's `LOD_SCREEN_SIZES`), where that's simpler than the camera's and the copy is on the GPU (`FarCascadeLods`; it draws the camera's mesh until then). The simpler copies are listed for upload an eighth of the meshes a frame (`FrameUses::add_lods`). Off, every cascade draws the camera's level, pixel for pixel as before. Overgrown Street, `owner-ultra`, 2560 × 1405, pinned, three alternating runs a view against origin/main f805d102: 40.6 → 42.1 FPS at the spawn (1% low 34.2 → 38.5), 40.8 → 42.1 (houseA), 40.0 → 41.1 (canopy), 31.0 → 31.6 (high), the farthest cascade 1.95–2.19 → 1.11–1.52 ms a frame on average and its frames' spikes lower (1% lows +5.6% to +12.6%); the frame's thread 0.04–0.30 ms longer. In the four stills 0.1–3.8% of pixels change, 0–0.004% by more than 8 levels of 255 (the most by 14). Test: `the_farthest_cascade_draws_casters_at_the_level_their_size_in_its_map_calls_for`.
- **Stand-ins in the farthest cascade** (2026-11-28): with Far shadow detail on, a caster whose level there is `STAND_IN_LEVEL` (2) or simpler is cast by its model's stand-in (`MeshSource::stand_in`, a `_PROXY` object: a generated tree's thick limbs and a few big crossed leaf cards, about 1,200 triangles and 4 cards deep from the side against 16 at full detail) once that's on the GPU, and a piece the stand-in covers is left out once it is (`StandInKey::mesh`). A stand-in's cut-out cards always cast through their holes, even past `cut_out_cascades`: cast whole, a few big cards would cast big squares. Stand-ins are listed for upload an eighth of the meshes a frame, as the simpler copies are (`FrameUses::add_stand_ins`), while Far shadow detail or a ray traced effect is on. `AXCENITY_NO_STAND_INS=1` uses the meshes drawn instead, for comparing. Only models with a `_PROXY` object (generated trees) change. Test: `the_farthest_cascade_casts_a_far_items_stand_in_and_leaves_out_what_it_covers`.
- **Under ray traced sun shadows** the nearest cascades wholly within the traced shadow's reach aren't drawn (2026-11-27): [ray-tracing.md](ray-tracing.md), "Cascades left out".
- `DrawItem::casts_shadow` (from a Part's or MeshPart's `CastShadow`, default true) leaves an item out of the shadow pass: it's still shaded by others. See-through items never cast. A cut-out casts through its texture's holes: its casters are grouped by material (`Caster::cut_out`) and drawn by the shadow pipeline for cut-outs (`vs_shadow_cut_out`, `fs_shadow_cut_out`, the material's bindings in group 1), so leaves, blades of grass and strands of hair cast their own shapes, not their cards'. Hair cards have since CO7 (2026-11-18), and hash their opacity round its cutoff against a fixed per-texel pattern, so the filter averages a strand's fringe into a partial shadow ([hair.md](hair.md)); every other cut-out of the standard material has since 2026-11-26. A shadow view draws its whole casters first and its cut-outs after them (a shadow map keeps the nearest depth whatever the order, and a plant's whole and cut-out parts would otherwise change the pipeline mesh by mesh). **Each view groups its casters by how they're drawn** (2026-11-26, `lighting::casters_in_views`): it sorts those that reach it by the material it draws them through (none for whole ones, first; a cut-out past the near cascades counts as whole) and mesh, so each mesh and material is one instanced draw. Until then a view took its casters in the scene's order, which starts with the camera's distance band, so a mesh's casters made a draw per band: Desert Homestead's front of the house by day drew its four cascades in 5,776 draws where they now take 1,695, the same maps pixel for pixel (`a_shadow_view_draws_each_mesh_once_whatever_distance_its_casters_are_at`; `results.md`). `RenderStats::cascade_draws` counts each cascade's draws. **Still casting their whole mesh**: a cut-out with a surface shader, terrain, or a texture tiled by its size (`uv_tile`, whose holes the shadow pass, reading the mesh's own coordinates, would put at the wrong scale). What it costs: the cut-out pipeline samples the texture and discards, so it has no early depth rejection, in every cascade. On `bench_cut_out_foliage_shadows` (render tests, ignored: 36,000 leaf cards under a low sun, 1080p High) the Shadows pass went from 0.19 to 0.42 ms, which is why foliage was left casting whole in 2026-11-18. In Desert Homestead at the owner's Ultra, 1920 × 1080 (six views, medians of three runs, 2026-11-26), the Shadows pass goes from 0.79 to 1.37 ms with whole cards to 1.13 to 1.51 ms through the holes: 0.13 to 0.17 ms more in five views and 0.42 ms at the windmill, 5 to 18 frames a second of 206 to 273. Drawing the whole casters first is worth 0.11 ms of that. `AXCENITY_NO_CUT_OUT_SHADOWS=1` puts whole cards back, to measure. **By cascade** (2026-11-26, the same views; `--bench` times each cascade): most of that is in the nearest cascades, where a plant covers the most texels and its holes show. At the windmill the 0.42 ms is 0.15 in the first cascade, 0.13 in the second, 0.10 in the third and 0.03 in the fourth. `ScenePipeline::cut_out_cascades` is how many of the sun's nearest cascades a cut-out other than a hair card casts through its holes in, and past them it casts whole (a hair card, and a lamp's view, always through the holes): every cascade as a renderer starts, or `AXCENITY_CUT_OUT_CASCADES=0` to `4`. It is left at every cascade because the near ones are where the time is: with 2 the Shadows pass is 0.09 to 0.14 ms shorter and blocks come back under plants past the second cascade's end (51 studs at Ultra to 300), 0.1 to 4.4% of a view's pixels, up to 1.5% of them by more than 16 levels of 255; with 3 it is 0.02 to 0.04 ms shorter and 0.02 to 0.75% of pixels change (`a_standard_cut_out_casts_whole_past_the_near_cascades_and_a_hair_card_does_not`). The same maps light what comes through a leaf from behind (`soft_sun_visibility`), with ray traced shadows on too, so with whole cards about 2% of such a picture showed card-shaped patches on the plants themselves. An item with a `cull_distance` (painted foliage) casts only to `SHADOW_REACH` (45%) of that distance from the camera (`renderer/lod.rs`, `ShadowReach`), so thousands of pebbles and grass tufts aren't drawn into every cascade where their shadows can't be told apart; it's still drawn past that, seated by ambient occlusion. **Its shadow thins out before it ends** (2026-11-26): whole to 35%, then less of it over `SHADOW_FADE` (10% of the cull distance) to none at 45% (`Draw::shadow`). Until then it went all at once at 35%, and every plant the camera moved across that line popped its shadow on or off. In the shadow maps a thinning caster goes through the cut-out pipeline whatever it is (the plain material for one with no holes, `CutOutCaster::NO_HOLES`), in every cascade, and `fs_shadow_cut_out` leaves that share of its texels out by `dithered_out`'s fixed pattern (the caster's copy of its instance holds the share where the scene's holds its fade), which the filter averages into a lighter shadow. Ray traced, a cut-out's slot says how much is left out (`CutOutRaw::casting`) and the shadow rays of that share of the pixels pass through it, by a pattern that moves on each frame under TAA (`casts_on_this_ray` in ray_shadows.wgsl); an item without holes, whose hits a ray can't pass over, casts until half of its shadow is gone. Measured in Desert Homestead's crane view, a step of 1% of the cull distance toward the plants changed 0.027% of the picture's pixels by more than 8 levels (the largest by 99) with the sudden end, and 0.003% (the largest by 25) with the fade; ray traced 0.027% and 95 against 0.001% and 29. What it costs: 1,700 to 2,400 more caster draws a view over the four cascades, 0.07 to 0.11 ms of the Shadows pass (`results.md`). `AXCENITY_SHADOW_REACH=reach,fade` tries others (`0.35,0` is the rule as it was). Tests: `a_plants_shadow_thins_out_before_the_end_of_its_reach` (both kinds of shadow), `a_plants_shadow_thins_out_over_the_band_before_its_reach`.
- Shadows fade out over the last 20% of `distance`. The sun's shadows darken only sun light (not ambient); a spot light's darken only its own light. Shadows off allocates no shadow map and skips the passes.
